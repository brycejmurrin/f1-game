THROTTLE

HOLD keeps your thumb on GAS. LATCH makes GAS a switch — tap once for full throttle, tap again to lift. AUTO drives the throttle for you so you only steer and brake — less tiring over a long race, and the way TOUCH steering has always worked.

LEFT-HANDED

Swaps the two on-screen thumb groups, so the pedals and the steering change hands.

GEARS

MANUAL needs free thumbs: tilt steering, a keyboard or a pad.

ACTIVE AERO

MANUAL: the AERO button is yours in every zone. AUTO: the wing opens itself, like the AI.

Using a keyboard or a controller with this device? Press any key, or any controller button, and its table appears here.

KEYBOARD

Tap a key slot, then press the key you want. Esc cancels. A key already used by another action moves here.

CONTROLLER

Tap a slot, then press a button on the controller. Esc cancels. The stick and D‑pad steer.

BUTTON NAMES

AUTO reads the name the controller reports, which not every pad gives us — pick your own if the prompts name the wrong buttons. A Switch‑style pad swaps A/B and X/Y against Xbox positions.

CALIBRATE STICK: let go of the stick, then press it — where it rests is subtracted from then on, which is the fix for a drifting stick that does not cost you fine steering. SET UP A WHEEL asks you to turn the wheel and press each pedal, for wheels the browser cannot map on its own.

      ADVANCED

      TILT INPUT how phone motion becomes steering (tilt mode)

      The same sensitivity as the top slider, with a finer label: how far you tilt the phone for full lock. Higher = less tilt needed; lower = relaxed, big tilts.

      How steady the tilt filter holds the wheel. Higher = smoother and calmer at rest (kills hand jitter) with a touch more lag; lower = more immediate.

      Trims LINEARITY for tilt only. Lower = gentler near level, for fine trim; higher = more direct. 5 leaves LINEARITY exactly as it is.

      TOUCH INPUT dragging on the track (touch mode)

      How far you drag for full lock. Higher = a shorter drag turns more (twitchier); lower = a long, deliberate sweep. Measured against the long edge of the screen, so it feels the same in either orientation.

      Filters thumb tremor out of a drag without dulling a flick. 1 is OFF (exactly as it always was). Raise it if the car wanders on a straight.

      CONTROLLER stick, d-pad and triggers

      Trims LINEARITY for the stick only. Lower = finer control near centre, which is what most pad players want; 5 leaves LINEARITY exactly as it is.

      Stick movement ignored around centre, as a percentage of travel. Raise it only if a resting stick drifts — every point costs you fine corrections. Use CALIBRATE first.

      How much travel short of the rim still counts as full lock. Raise it if a worn stick can no longer reach full steering.

      KEYS & ON-SCREEN ARROWS digital steering

      How fast a held key or arrow winds on lock. Higher = quicker to full lock and twitchier; lower = steadier and easier to place. Letting go always unwinds at twice the rate you wind on.

      CAR HANDLING how steering becomes motion (all inputs)

      How much of each corner the car steers for you. Higher = the car tracks the bend automatically and you just trim the line; lower = you do the work. The single biggest “easier to drive” knob.

      Turn-in snappiness (shorter wheelbase). Higher = sharper, faster turn-in; lower = lazier and more stable.

      Input curve. Low = gentle near centre for fine corrections; high = direct, 1:1 response. Raise it if steering feels mushy near centre.

      Maximum turn angle at full lock. Higher = tighter cornering, but easier to over-rotate. (Auto-tapers at high speed.)

      How much steering you keep at high speed. Higher = sharper and more responsive flat-out; lower = calmer and more stable.

      Softens tilt, a drag and the stick as you go faster, so the flick that places the car in a hairpin is not a spin at the end of a straight. 1 is OFF; full keeps 40% of your steering flat out. ADAPTIVE BUTTONS already does this for keys and arrows.

      How heavy the car feels as it rotates. Higher = more planted and settled, and it takes longer to swing into a corner; lower = lighter and snappier. 5 is the standard car.

      DRIVER AID

      Optional bias toward the line. PULL (+) eases toward the apex; PUSH (−) runs wider; OFF = pure manual. Never overrides your steering.

      HOW TO PLAY

      CONTROLS RACING PIT STOPS DRIVING SETUP MODES FRIENDS

      QUICK START

      Wait for lights out, accelerate, then brake before turning. Ease off the brake as you steer and add throttle as the car straightens.

      KEYBOARD

      Steer ← → / A D · Gas ↑ / W · Brake ↓ / S · Boost Space · Overtake X · Active aero Z · Camera C · Pause P

      Change driving keys in SETTINGS › CONTROLS › KEYBOARD. In menus, arrows move, Enter confirms and Esc goes back. Esc pauses when no menu is open.

      CONTROLLER

      Steer left stick / D-pad · Gas RT / A · Brake LT / B · Boost X · Overtake Y · Active aero D-pad ↑ · Camera View · Pause Menu / Start

      Triggers give gradual throttle and braking. Change driving buttons and prompt labels in SETTINGS › CONTROLS › CONTROLLER. In menus, use either stick or the D-pad; the confirm button selects, the back button returns and triggers page.

      TOUCH / MOBILE

      Choose SETTINGS › CONTROLS › STEERING INPUT. Rotate to landscape to drive.

      TILT: roll the phone to steer; use GAS and BRAKE. It calibrates at the lights. If motion permission is denied, the game switches to BUTTONS.

      BUTTONS: hold an on-screen arrow and slide back to ease steering. Pedals sit opposite the arrows.

      TOUCH: drag from where your finger lands on the track. Gas is automatic; use BRAKE to slow down. A tap alone does not steer.

      CONTROLS also has pedal layout and handedness. DISPLAY lets you size the interface, HUD and touch buttons separately.

      BOOST
      Toggle electric power on or off. It drains the energy bar, which recharges when you lift or brake. Power tapers at very high speed.
      ACTIVE AERO
      In MANUAL, press your active aero control (keyboard Z, controller D-pad ↑, or AERO on screen) inside an activation zone. X-MODE reduces drag and grip; braking or leaving the zone closes the wings.
      SETTINGS › CONTROLS › ACTIVE AERO: AUTO handles the wings for you. The HUD shows the automatic mode and zone status. Zones are available on every lap with no proximity rule; some circuits have none.
      OVERTAKE
      Follow within one second to arm it, then activate for four seconds of electric power. The HUD explains when it is unavailable: the opening lap, a caution or the cooldown. When ready, it shows OVERTAKE READY. Overtake becomes available when the leader starts lap two.
      GEARS
      Choose SETTINGS › CONTROLS › GEARS: MANUAL to shift yourself. Shift up near the redline and down before slow corners. Manual gears work on desktop and with TILT on a phone; BUTTONS and TOUCH keep gears automatic.
      PIT STOPS — TYRE WEAR RUNS THEM
      SETTINGS → TYRE WEAR ships on LIGHT: tyres wear at a little over half the rate the compounds are built around, so a set lasts roughly twice as long and stopping is a choice rather than a schedule. REAL wears them at the full rate — a set will not reach the flag and a stop is part of the race.
      Turn it to OFF and there is no pit lane at all — nothing painted on the road, no prompt, no stop, and the AI never pits either
      THERE IS NO PIT BUTTON
      You call a stop the way a driver does: put the car in the pit lane at the entry and keep it there for about half a second. The limiter comes on by itself and the crew tells you which set is ready. A car that merely runs wide at the entry, or is spun or stopped there, calls nothing — it has to be deliberate and held
      FINDING THE LANE
      The lane is the strip of road on the pit side with the blue line along its inner edge, and it exists only for the last few hundred metres before the start/finish line. A blue bar across its mouth marks where it begins — that is what you steer into.
      The HUD counts it down for you — PIT 340m with an arrow to the pit side, then PIT ENTRY once you are on the entry road. It deliberately stays quiet until a stop is worth making, so on a fresh set you will see nothing at all
      THE LANE AND THE BOX
      Inside the lane you are limited to 80 km/h (60 on a street circuit) — the limiter is automatic, you do not switch it on. Drive down to your team’s box and stop there: each team has its own, spread along the lane, and team-mates share one.
      You have to stop IN the lane. Halt on the racing line and the stop does not happen — the crew is not standing there. Coming up to the box the HUD says KEEP RIGHT (or LEFT) if you have drifted out. Blowing through at the limit misses it too: you have to actually stop.
      The stop itself is 2.4 s, then TYRES ON — GO GO GO
      WHAT IT COSTS
      The whole lap through the pits, against racing the same stretch, plus the time stood still — which is why it is worth different amounts at different circuits rather than a fixed penalty. A long pit straight costs more than a short one. That difference is what decides one stop against two
      DRIVING COACH
      Open SETTINGS → DRIVING and turn the coach ON. Short text tips appear in the race-message area while you drive. The coach watches sustained braking, steering and tyre-slip conditions; it never changes your controls. Your ON/OFF choice is saved.
      Where the lap went. Once you have a saved best lap on a circuit, each completed lap is compared with it corner by corner, and the corner that cost the most time is named when you cross the line. The DRIVING page shows the lap total and the two worst corners. Each figure covers a corner and the straight after it, because a slow straight is usually paid for at the apex before it, and the comparison only exists against your own best lap in the same car.
      It waits during flags, other race messages and pit stops, and spaces tips out so brief corrections do not cause a stream of messages. Reopen DRIVING to read the latest tip and its explanation. SESSION REVIEW counts the tips recorded this session; those counts are not a driving score.
      WHAT THE TIPS MEAN
      Braking into a turn: release the brake gradually as you add steering.
      Front tyres sliding: reduce steering angle to help the tyres regain grip.
      Rear tyres sliding: the coach distinguishes accelerating, braking and coasting, so the suggested correction matches your inputs.
      Off the track: ease your inputs and rejoin carefully. The coach does not know whether there is a safe gap in traffic.
      Coasting: neither pedal was used at speed for over a second. Be on the brake or the throttle.
      X-mode in corners: the active aero was open while cornering hard. Close it before you turn in.
      Track limits: a warning was recorded for leaving the track. Four in a row cost five seconds.
      PRACTICE A SECTION
      Start a solo TIME TRIAL. Drive to a useful starting point, open SETTINGS → DRIVING, expand PRACTICE A SECTION, choose a PRACTICE GOAL, then SAVE STARTING POINT. Resume and drive the section. TRY AGAIN, or your RECOVER key (R by default) while driving, restores the saved grid, race clock and practice goal.
      This makes the session unscored. Laps and ghosts will not be saved after a starting point is set. Restart the session to set records again. A solo race or qualifying session can use checkpoints after you choose ARM PRACTICE; daily challenges and multiplayer cannot be armed.
      PRACTICE GOALS
      Free practice: repeat any section. Sector: cross the next sector line cleanly. Corner: drive through the next corner; the result shows your time, minimum speed and exit speed. Full lap: cross the line to start timing, then complete a clean lap; practice laps are never saved, so this is how to time one. Braking: build speed before saving, then brake firmly and keep braking until the car stops; a tap followed by coasting does not count, and the result is your stopping distance, plus how many metres of it were spent below the hardest braking the car managed on that attempt. Trail braking: brake firmly, keep some brake on as the car turns in, then release it while the car is still turning. Slalom: make six direction changes while moving; the car must change direction, not just the stick. Launch: stop the car before saving, then launch to racing speed, timed from your first throttle. Each goal judges what the car did and shows the result on screen when it ends. Contact or leaving the track marks an attempt for retry; clean completions are saved separately from lap records, and the DRIVING page shows this session's attempts and your saved best.
      PIT STRATEGY
      With tyre wear enabled, SETTINGS → DRIVING → PIT STRATEGY → NEXT TYRES selects the compound fitted at your next stop; AUTO lets the game choose. Enter the pit lane yourself. Pit loss, tyre life and energy use are estimates based on the circuit and recent driving. More clean sectors and laps improve the available data.
      SESSION REVIEW
      Review coaching tip counts, recent incidents and penalties after a run. Tips are listed most-repeated first and grouped by the turn they happened at, so one corner earning most of them is easy to see; when the same mistake comes up three times the review names the practice goal that drills it. Circuits without curated turn numbers simply show no location. TECHNICAL DATA contains the optional DOWNLOAD SESSION DATA JSON file and AI passing decisions. You do not need these tools to use the coach.
      TRACK LIMITS
      Repeatedly leaving the track gives three warnings, then a +5 s penalty on the fourth cut. The warning count then resets. The HUD shows warnings in every driving camera.
      CAMERA
      Tap the camera button or press your camera binding (keyboard C, controller View) to cycle views. Hold the on-screen button briefly, or right-click it, to open the picker. Your choice is saved.
      SETTINGS
      Pause with your binding (keyboard P, controller Menu / Start) or the pause button, then open SETTINGS. You can also open it from the title menu. BACK returns to the previous page.
      CONTROLS
      Choose input, pedals, gears and active aero here. Rebind keyboard and controller actions, calibrate a controller, or set up a wheel. A keyboard or connected controller can be used alongside touch controls.
      STEERING & ASSISTS
      Choose response presets, driving aids and the racing line here. ADAPTIVE BUTTONS changes keys and on-screen arrows at speed; it does not alter the analog stick. BRAKE CUE is an audio hint. Expand ADVANCED for detailed steering adjustments.
      DISPLAY
      Adjust UI, HUD and button sizes separately. HUD settings control layout, map and visibility; the eye icon restores a hidden HUD. During a race, ADVANCED VISUALS opens the lighting and camera tuners with a live preview.
      MUSIC & SOUND
      Choose music and effects, their volumes and playback controls in SETTINGS › MUSIC & SOUND.
      RACE
      22 cars, five red lights, then lights out. Points go to the first ten: 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1.
      SEASON
      Race the calendar in order. Championship progress is saved between visits.
      TIME TRIAL
      Drive solo for your fastest lap. Your track record is saved and shown on the circuit list.
      CAREER
      Sign for a team as a DRIVER or run your own in MY TEAM. Qualify, race, earn prize money and develop the car. Each mode has three save slots and its own guide under CAREER MODES.
      QUALIFYING
      Championship weekends start with a flying lap. DRIVE MY LAP sets your own time; SIMULATE calculates one. The result sets your starting grid.
      RACE A FRIEND
      Create an invite, send it to your friend and paste their reply to connect. In the same room, you can scan QR codes instead. Everyone needs the same game version.
