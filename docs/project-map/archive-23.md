## UI mechanism and test contracts

Sources 3–7 describe related stages of one investigation, with different evidentiary roles. Source 3 records height-budget failures: garage chrome consumed 76% of the sheet, circuit facts escaped an unscrolled clipper, fixed chip tracks truncated their own labels, the garage panel and camera bar disagreed about zoomed width, and title/data surfaces missed parts of the sheet system. These are dated measurements, not an assertion that the current UI still has those defects.

Source 4 names list-detail/supporting-pane patterns and separates viewport size/shape, container size/shape, input modality, density, safe areas and display segments. The document initially calls these seven axes although its expanded table has eight. A content-sized sheet cannot use two-axis size containment without changing its visual sizing. Its adopted alternative measures once through ResizeObserver and exposes shape and pair attributes. Pair thresholds belong to one authority: leaving both a container query and an observer attribute in charge creates a transient broken layout. ID-specific stacked rules can defeat a class primitive; the recorded repair gates those rules on the absence of the split state, allowing an unmeasured sheet to stack safely.

The same source records the top-layer migration seam: the existing hidden attribute remains authoritative, with a watcher mirroring it to native dialog open/close. Escape must invoke each screen's existing leave control so that lobby camera/connection teardown, pause/settings nesting and race-setting cancellation survive. Results refuses generic dismissal. These are historical behavior contracts and reported checks, not fresh verification. The chronological progress notes move from 12 migrated modals to all sixteen, and from a proposed pair primitive to garage/career adoption with select still pending at that point.

Source 7 measures the zoom costs precisely. Percentages follow the already-scaled parent; viewport units resolve against the unzoomed viewport and then multiply. Separate UI and HUD inverse-unit tokens compensate their respective scopes. A variable consumed in both spaces needs correction at consumption rather than declaration. The coordinate-space issue concerns mixed spaces, not merely a rect near zoom: a telemetry candidate was withdrawn because both coordinates were unzoomed. Historical Safari rect behavior and zoom/container-query interoperability could not be observed by the Chromium-only suite. Proposed feature detection, currentCSSZoom tolerance and a viewport rect helper were research suggestions, not proof of current adoption.

Source 5 must be read with its revision. The original claim that tap targets did not scale was false: consumption inside zoom already enlarged them. Its first replacement unit would keep real-pixel container thresholds fixed while content grew; the revision uses container-relative em thresholds with a scale-driven container font. It anchors the unit to rem, makes touch floors independent of scaling, revises the type ladder to six hand-set rungs, and warns that increasing the floor changes visible row counts. The in-game 80–150% slider does not itself establish 200% text-resizing conformance. A proposed global zoom removal becomes a per-scope migration gated by zero unallowlisted raw lengths. Proposed lints and token names remain proposals here.

Source 6 narrows the decision after measurement: fix local user-visible defects, favor subtractive vocabulary/token work, defer changing the scaling mechanism until migration coverage is enforceable. Its two hard findings in 380 cells were the two tuner manifestations of one cause. It rejects shell partial fetching, screen-inventory churn and methodology renames on that evidence. Its class-count ratchet was prescribed, not proved installed; a later live research record explicitly says it had not been installed and corrects the meaning of the screen count.

## Instrument corrections are part of the evidence

Source 2's implementation box supersedes several assertions in its body. Six recommendations are marked implemented; 126 sub-floor font literals were migrated to zero, while raw spacing fell from 529 to 479. A fifth zero-spacing-token sheet was found by a guard; the responsive sheet legitimately retains viewport-absolute values. An alleged 18 pre-existing test failures were missing dependencies; the corrected result was 426 passing. The original map explanation also had to be reconciled with a deploy-branch rewrite. The retained fix prevents inline sizing from pinning a hidden card and observes its laid-out size; attribute visibility alone occurs before layout settles, and a fixed rAF retry budget failed during View Transitions.

The same record demonstrates that stopping rendering can delay rAF and observer delivery by seconds. An audit under that condition can report pre-observer state as final. A map judged a cosmetic 80×119 metric was actually permanently tiny in the ordinary live loop; the correction gave a second fit after the card became measurable. The record also corrects its own dpr-1 recommendation: DevTools screenshots were unreliable at multiple dpr values, so geometry should be independently inspected and captures taken with a trustworthy instrument. Emulation sometimes reloads app state and sometimes does not; check the open screen before measuring.

Source 4 recalibrates house touch-floor warnings separately from WCAG's 24px minimum. It corrects a sweep that serialized a function expression instead of evaluating the intended probe and thus measured nothing. It replaces curated scroll-region names with computed overflow and real scroll range on either axis; horizontal strips and ordinary scroll divs had produced false unreachable-control claims. Source 2 adds another trap: collapsed details can retain descendant boxes while the pseudo-element suppresses visibility, so ancestor walks and rectangles alone were insufficient. The reliable lesson across these sources is to corroborate a new probe's finding independently.

Historical regression contracts include CSS layer-boundary checks, token-adoption ratchets, both-orientation UI-scale overflow checks, geometry audits, ARIA structural snapshots and six menu pixel baselines. Source 4 reports deliberately changing brand red to blue to prove the baselines could fail. Source 7 measures canvas dither at essentially the existing pixel tolerance and suggests removing the game canvas from menu captures to reduce noise. Source 2 distinguishes structural ARIA regression coverage from geometry coverage: an accessible tree cannot catch a map overflowing its card. No listed pass count, browser support date or implementation status was rerun here.
