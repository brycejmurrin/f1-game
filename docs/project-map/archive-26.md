## Historical contracts and findings

S01 treats tests as executable claims whose failure must mean something observable. Strong examples include independent QR decoding, seeded local-versus-fed multiplayer trajectories, reliable-channel survival under loss, identity-based rival routing, unknown-packet rejection, same-instant race starts, lamp dwell without backfill, camera-track release, and persistence surviving storage failure through a cache while logging a named warning. It distinguishes unit/integration layering from duplicate coverage: codec versus posed-car behavior, math versus telemetry UI, and catalog versus actual rendered factory meshes.

Physics contracts include brake/coast/throttle ordering, grass drag, steering sign and driver authority, lap-seam accounting, world-to-track projection, relative wall scrub, PACE invariance, deterministic characterization across commits, rescue with held versus released throttle, ERS pushing and draining across speed, and positive controls before asserting haptic silence. These are dated descriptions of intended protections, not confirmation that each named historical test remains present.

The appendix diagnoses structural cost separately from assertion value. Circuit foundation tests were grouped as physics despite testing elevation, required models, overhead clearance, terrain grounding, banking and barriers. Proposed regrouping would both stop unrelated driving changes paying for circuit builds and ensure circuit changes select those protections. It also proposes separating hook contracts from foundation diagnostics, car catalog/modifier behavior from geometry, and heading physics from input-latch tests. Proposed filenames are design suggestions rather than present files.

S01's repeated weak patterns are useful review questions: screenshot galleries counted as regression coverage; typeof-only hooks; duplicated expected algorithms; accepting missing terrain or vertices; skipped circuits after failed staging; broad literal bounds; stale counts; sleeping instead of waiting for state; and reporting hook state instead of actual shader uploads. Concrete concerns include the blanket high-road exemption masking defects outside crossover circuits, map orientation claimed without a landmark assertion, contrast tests checking their own picker, and race-control cap tests never exercising cap expiry. Dormant shimmer and track-baseline suites explicitly prove nothing until exercised; software-renderer timing and PNG size heuristics are limited evidence.

S02 organizes its synthesis into immediate behavior fixes, low-level cleanup, docs/tools work, restructure companions and deferrals. Its largest corrective cluster is the curvature/right-vector convention: labels had been corrected while kerbs, tyre barriers, corner boards and camera controls allegedly still consumed the opposite sign. Other report clusters cover full-career-slot selection, qualifying Escape behavior, invite hashes on reload, caution caps, incident handback speed, unreachable drizzle controls, remote-player results markers, remote music resume, fraction-versus-metre agent descriptions, scenery emission errors, material-texture defaults, wet-road SSR consumption, lobby/session ownership and live-data cache freshness.

The structured opening provides claim/evidence/fix fields plus skeptic/refutation counts for the game core and early game modules. It distinguishes unused façade exports from local functions still needed internally, records stale cosmetic-key and camera-distance prose, and traces lighting persistence to its extracted owner. The report's aggregate claim of zero net refutations coexists with a camera finding recording one attempted refutation: the narrative says it was subsequently upheld, so these fields should not be flattened into a claim that no objection occurred.

S02 preserves sequencing rationale: functional fixes to broken audit routes should precede mechanical test moves; launch-path and output-resolution repairs could accompany tool relocation; panel controls should move with their owner. Deferrals include racing/source coordinate compensation requiring a joint engine/circuit migration, WGSL specification uncertainty, art and feature decisions, cache eviction, and duplicated VM/server harnesses. Those are historical decisions, not permission to execute them today.
