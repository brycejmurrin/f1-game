# Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya — Visual Design Brief

**Setting:** DAY, modern theme (dry Catalan hillside at Montmeló). ~4.66 km, 16 turns, clockwise.

## 1. Setting
Cut into open farmland hillside 20 km north of Barcelona, above the Montmeló plain. This is the circuit every team has tested on for thirty years, and it looks it: long clean sightlines, enormous run-off, no trees to speak of, and a low white pit terrace whose horizontal louvred facade reads as Mediterranean from a hundred metres away. The outfield is dry terraced farmland cut into pale rock, not woodland. The Montseny massif closes the inland horizon.

## 2. Atmosphere & palette
Hard dry sun, bleached everything. Catalunya has no grey steel and no dark timber — the built world here is white render, sun-baked concrete and pale ochre stone.

## 3. Elevation
A hillside lap: it climbs from Turn 1 to the high ground at Campsa, then falls all the way back.
- s≈0.00–0.06: flat 700 m main straight into **Elf** (T1).
- s≈0.18: **7 m climb** through Renault and Repsol.
- s≈0.44: high ground before **Campsa** (~11 m up) — the crest the car goes light over.
- s≈0.62: **9 m drop** off Campsa toward La Caixa.
- s≈0.86: continued 6 m descent to the final chicane and the line.

## 5. Track features
- 700 m main straight into a heavy, downhill-braking Turn 1 — the only real overtaking place.
- Cambered turns: Elf 3.5°, Repsol 4°, **Campsa 4.5° over a crest**, Europcar 3°.
- Three pinch points: the Seat/Würth chicane, La Caixa hairpin, and the final chicane complex.
- Vast ochre asphalt-and-gravel run-off; kerbs low and flat, nothing sausage-shaped.

## 6. Modelling notes
- The louvred pit facade is the circuit's architectural signature — build it as stacked horizontal shading bands, not a wall with a window strip painted on.
- Crowd terracing is UNCOVERED raked concrete with shade sails, not a clad grandstand shell. Only Turn 1 gets a roof.
- Keep planting thin and low. A dense tree belt anywhere would kill the long sightlines that define this track.
- Carry the outfield with pale ochre terrace benches stepping up the slope — that dry-farmland backdrop is what stops Catalunya looking German.
- Make the masts genuinely tall and slim; they are the only strong verticals on the whole site.
- Leave the infield inside Campsa and La Caixa empty. Openness is a feature here, not an omission.
