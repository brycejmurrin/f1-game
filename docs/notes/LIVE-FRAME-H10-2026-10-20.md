# Live race frame time at the phone shape (B·H10, 2026-10-20)

Lane B·H10 (Fleet B, Bryce 2026-10-20: "right now it feels a little laggy"). The question: in a race,
as a phone player gets it, where do frame time and stutter go?

Live build: `2eda36c6` (= deploy tip, nothing unpublished; `version.json` build 19972).

## What a phone binds

`Gfx.create` (`js/render/gfx.js:265`) sends every unset backend to TLX. Inside TLX
(`js/render/three/tlx.js:385`), WebKit takes three's **WebGL2** backend on AUTO and other phones
take three's **WebGPU** backend with lite caps. Bryce's phone is an iPhone 15
(`UI-AUDIT-ALL-2026-10-18.md`), so he gets **TLX on three-WebGL2**, mobile tier, DPR capped at 1.5
(`js/render/glx/glx.js:1009`), GRAPHICS MEDIUM (`js/perf/quality-preset.js:51`). An Android phone gets
TLX on three-WebGPU.

## Instrument

`scratch/h10-live-frame.mjs` on the measurement branch `cursor/live-frame-h10-census` (census request leg
`h10-live-frame`, not for merging). Each row is a fresh browser at 844x390 css x 1.5 with touch and
GRAPHICS MEDIUM. It does not set `forceMobileTier`, which loses the WebGPU device on the paravirtual
runner (`TLX-GPU-COST-PARAVIRTUAL-2026-10-20.md` §3). The camera is helmet (the default). The car drives
under the census closed-loop law for 8 s, then a 40 s window records:

- rAF interval and the game's rAF-callback wall;
- PerfSpans per phase and long-animation frames;
- 6 s of CDP trace: GC events and merged per-thread toplevel busy time.

Rev 2 adds a recorder for every GL or WebGPU call over 20 ms, with the bound program's
`SHADER_NAME` and a stack, plus counts of program links and pipeline creations inside the window.

macos-latest is an Apple **paravirtual** GPU with no `timestamp-query`. Its absolute frame times are the
runner's present path, not a phone's (§2 of the H7 note). The rows below are read for *structure*: a
synchronous call that blocks the thread, a long task, a GC pause. They are not read as fill cost.

Read:

- **TLX-WebGL2, the iPhone path: one frame per race blocks the main thread for 34–102 s after the race
  warm**, on all three tracks. On monaco and spa the stall is inside the game's rAF callback (the
  long-animation frame is all blocking script). In the 6 s trace the GPU process main thread
  (`CrGpuMain`) was busy for 67–79 s. That is a synchronous GL call waiting on GPU-process work, and a
  program link or first-draw pipeline build is the usual cause on ANGLE-Metal (see `tlx.js:3080`, the
  draw warm). The paravirtual device stretches it to tens of seconds. On a phone the same event is a
  visible hitch, not a minute.
- **TLX-WebGPU: 6–9 % of frames run over 100 ms with 6–37 ms of script and no blocking time.** These
  are present or GPU waits, and on this runner they cannot be told apart from the paravirtual swapchain.
- The game's own JS per frame is 5–6 ms p50 on WebGPU and 10–13 ms on WebGL2 (render span 2.6–5.2 vs
  7.5–10 ms, so three's WebGL2 backend costs about 2x the CPU of its WebGPU backend). Physics is
  0.9–1.5 ms p50 and 3–8 ms p95, the p95 being two steps plus GC.
- The governor drops these rows to render scale 0.5 (633x293 present). That is the runner's slowness, not a
  phone reading.

## Rounds 2–6 — naming the TLX-WebGL2 stall

Same leg, same shape. The artifact `h10-live-frame-macos-latest` carries `rows[].slow`: slow GL/WebGPU calls,
link and pipeline counts, and `pipeNames`, every `backend.createRenderPipeline` three r186 makes, with its
material, object, userData keys, attributes, target, governor tier and page time.

| rev | run | knobs | in-window links (spa / singapore) | longest block |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 39508010962 | default | 9 / 10 (monaco 0) | `getProgramParameter` 124 s (singapore) |
| 3 | 39509923950 | default | 5 / 5 | world chunk into `output`, 84 s |
| 5 | 39510807053 | `apex26.tlxDrawWarm=1` | 1 / 5 | world chunk 156 s; `bufferData` 17.7 s in `createBindings` (spa) |
| 6 | 39510922959 | default, plus tier log | 3 / 0 | world chunk 19.8 s, 25 s after the last tier change |

What the rows say:

- **Every in-window link draws into `output`**, the post chain's scene target (`tlx-post.js:211`
  `sceneRT.texture.name = "output"`). The warm built the same world-chunk layout (attrs
  `position,normal,color,mat`; userData `tlxEmissive,tlxAlpha,tlxLgRoad`) for `canvas`, the mirror
  (211x60) and the sun shadow, but **never for `output`**, in every run, with or without the draw warm.
- **The governor is not the trigger.** In rev 6 the spa link came 25 s after the last tier change.
- **It depends on the route.** Singapore had 5, 5 and then 0 links across runs. Each one is a chunk variant
  entering the view for the first time.
- **The same family appears as a first-bindings stall:** `bufferData` under
  `yR.updateBindings < createBindings < _createBindings < getForRender` blocked for 17.7 s in rev 5. That is a
  render object's first draw allocating its uniform buffer.
- The CPU profiles (rev 4, run 39510599616) are swamped by the same syncs: `bindBuffer` 14.4 s,
  `blendFuncSeparate` 70.6 s. Each is a GL call that waits on the GPU process while it finishes a pending
  link. Outside them, three's WebGL2 backend runs about 4–7 ms/frame (`_renderObjectDirect`, `updateForRender`), `game.js`
  about 0.9, `tlx.js` 0.4, `hud-layout.js` plus `getBoundingClientRect` about 0.7.

On ANGLE-Metal and WebKit the same first-draw work happens. The paravirtual device stretches it to seconds,
and a phone shows it as a hitch the first time each chunk variant comes into view. **Handed to B·H8** (owner of
the TLX warm). Its fix is to compile against `post.sceneTarget()` with frustum culling off for the warm, so
off-camera chunk variants are built at the lights.
