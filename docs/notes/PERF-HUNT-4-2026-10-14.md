## Target 1: the opening-frame stall

**Facts from the JSON**

1. The opening frames are rAF gaps, not long callbacks:
   - `open.maxMs` values: 4116, 3867, 2217, 2583, 2300 and 1917 ms.
   - The hitch recorder's callbacks in the same window are at most 120–160 ms.
   - The beats keep answering every second through the gap.
   - `spikeCpu` samples (idle) at 100%.
2. The gaps fall in the first 2–5 frames that draw after the warm:

   | leg | frame dts after the warm (ms) |
   |---|---|
   | montreal WebGL2 | 117, 117, 4117, then ~3017 |
   | montreal WebGPU | 100, 1550, 3867 |
   | vegas WebGPU | 67, 2050, 2583, 1700 |
   | spa WebGL2 | 38, 2300 |
   | spa WebGPU | 117, 1133, 1267, 1917 |

   The UBO / render-object count climbs through the gaps: 358 → 422 on montreal WebGL2, 303 → 371 on montreal WebGPU, 391 → 502 on spa WebGPU. The warm (`tlx.js:2715-2832`) only calls `compileAsync`, and `tickBody` returns while warming (`game.js:8424`). So these frames are the first real draws of everything.
3. **On WebGPU, no shader module or pipeline was created in that window** (exact wrapped counts; `compileStacks` is `[]`). The `_coWarm` hypothesis is therefore refuted on WebGPU.
   - The scene warm starts on the first race present, after `_showInstanced` has made the grid's real coalesced runs visible (`tlx.js:4931-4988`).
   - So `compileAsync(scene)` compiles the real `|ai` and `|ds|ai` wheel variants on their real layouts, and `_coWarm` exits early (`:2398`).
   - Per-key materials differ only in uniform packs (`tsl-lit.js:2337-2349`, `pinProgram`), and three keys programs by shader text.
4. GLX and WGX also have multi-second opening frames on every track (GLX 2.18 / 2.42 / 1.62 s, WGX 3.40 / 3.18 / 2.23 s). Hunt 3's "1.8 → 4.1 s worse" is a single max sample on a VM; GLX and WGX moved +0.3 and +0.5 s on the same runs with no code change.

**Root cause, ranked**

- **(a) Driver work at the first draw that `compileAsync` cannot reach.** On ANGLE-Metal the MTLRenderPipelineState (program × vertex descriptor × formats × blend × samples) is created at draw time; ANGLE's `setupDraw` checks for and creates it (WebKit bug 246777).
- **(b) WebGL2 only: ANGLE-Metal converts vertex buffers whose stride or offset is not 4-byte aligned, at the first draw.** It does this in the GPU process, on the CPU for half-float formats (`VertexArrayMtl.mm` `syncDirtyAttrib` `needConversion`; `convertVertexBufferCPU`). Metal requires 4-byte strides (Mozilla bug 2052528; Dawn validates the same rule). **Measured:** montreal's WebGL2 pack has 6 layouts with **12.6 of 30.4 MB in non-4-byte strides** (normal Int16x3n, colour Uint8x3n / Float16x3, mat Float16x1); WebGPU has 3 layouts and 0 MB.
- **(c) WebGPU:** no compile was counted. The candidates are Dawn/Metal first use of resources, residency, or the paravirtual device's own deferred work. These cannot be separated from the JSON; it needs a real Mac.

**Proposed fixes**

- **W1, a draw warm (S–M, identical pixels).** Add a last stage in `startProgramWarm`, after the mirror stage at `tlx.js:2818`, inside the try:
  - `renderer.autoClear = false`; `setRenderTarget(post.sceneTarget())`; set the MRT exactly as present does; `setScissorTest(true)`; `setScissor(0, 0, 1, 1)`; `render(scene, camera)`.
  - The same once for `mirRT` with the mirror views shown (`_mirWarmSwap`), one sun cast, and one `post.present` under the scissor. Then restore.
  - Record `warm.draw` in `_warmStages`.
  - Expected: `gov.open.maxMs` falls and `warm.total` rises by about the same amount; montreal WebGL2 4.1 s → a normal frame, warm +2–4 s.
- **W2, 4-byte strides on WebGL2 (S, identical).** In `packAttr` / `buildGeometry` (`tlx-chunked.js:113-230`, `tlx.js:3205-3209`), apply a pad rule like fmt24 on three's WebGL backend:
  - normal Int16x3n → Int16x4n (the existing `pad4`)
  - mat Float16x1 → Float32
  - colour Uint8x3n → Uint8x4n. tsl-lit reads `attribute("color","vec3")` (`tsl-lit.js:1322`), but audit every other colour reader first: the comment at `tlx-chunked.js:85-92` says three's `vertexColor` reads a 4-wide colour as RGBA.
  - Cost: +5 B per vertex, about +4.8 MB on montreal. Verify with `layouts.cjs`, expecting non4MB = 0.
- **Census instrument gaps (S, tools only).**
  - (i) WebGL2 legs report "no resource calls observed". Wrap `WebGL2RenderingContext.prototype.linkProgram`, `bufferData`, `bufferSubData`, `texImage*` and `generateMipmap`.
  - (ii) `analyseSpikeWork` keys on callback duration ≥100 ms, so it never sees a 4 s rAF gap. Add a gap-keyed view: the work done in the frame before each interval ≥500 ms.

## Target 4: post chain on the phone tier

At MEDIUM (userTier 2), `game.js:8323-8336` zeroes `po.reflect` and `po.carReflect`; SSAO, godray and bloom stay until autoTier 4. Passes per frame by day: about 10.
- half-res: AO-in-godray + 2 blurs; bright + 4 down + 4 up bloom
- full-res: composite; FXAA + mirror inset

Full-res traffic at 0.74 MP on a tile GPU:
- scene store: RGBA16F 8 B/px + depth 4 B/px (stored, because AO and godray sample it)
- composite: reads 8, writes 4
- FXAA: reads 4, writes 4
- total ≈32 B/px ≈ **24 MB/frame** (≈1.4 GB/s at 60 Hz); half-res passes add about 25%.

- **P1 (S, identical).** When ssao, godray and reflect are all 0 (autoTier 4, or night), nothing samples the scene depth. Rebuild `sceneRT` (`tlx-post.js:186-190`) with a non-sampled depth, like R3's tagless rebuild via `_postRebuild`. On WebGL2, invalidate it after the scene pass (GLX does, `post.js:550`; MDN guidance). Saves ≈3 MB/frame (12%).
- **P2 (S, look change).** `BLOOM_LEVELS_MAX` 5 → 4 on `isMobile` (`tlx-post.js:222`) removes 2 near-empty passes (80×45 and 40×22 class). Each one is a fixed load/store plus, on WebGPU, an encoder and submit.
- **P3.** The phone mirror is the lite rung but still builds a full mip chain: ~8 extra passes per frame on WebGPU phones (H5).
- CPU: 35 µs per pass, so the post chain is ≈0.35 ms here; nothing new.

## Deferred designs

- **D1:** W1 behind `apex26.tlxDrawWarm`, off on software adapters (`skipBatches` / `softGpu`), after the instrument fixes land.
- **D2:** the ring run (H2).
- **D3, fewer WebGPU submits.** three submits per `render()` because `queue.writeBuffer` runs before the next submit, so deferring submits would let a later pass's uniform write corrupt an earlier pass. The only safe batching is a vendor patch: turn intra-frame writes into encoder-ordered `copyBufferToBuffer` from a mapped staging ring (webgpufundamentals pattern), then submit once per frame. L; only if a real Mac shows submit-bound frames.
- **D4:** wheels as one `BatchedMesh` multi-draw (r186 fixed draw offsets, #34212): about −37 RO, ≈1.1 ms on Mac WebGL2. L.
- **D5:** W2 interleaved instead of padded: normal Int16x4n + colour Uint8x4n in one 12 B buffer, mat as a separate Float32; +3 B per vertex.

## Summary

The most important result is about the census, not the game. **The real-GPU census does not measure GLX or WGX the way a player runs them, and its GLX pixel verdicts compare one stale frame.** Two consequences:
- The "spa dusk: GLX at tier 4, WGX at scale 0.5" result is not evidence that dusk costs more on the GPU.
- That question cannot be answered from fps or tier on this runner.

What the census does show, in four points:
1. **GLX's measured GPU time stays well below the frame time.**
   - GPU time is 10.5–16.8 ms per frame, against a frame of 50–66 ms.
   - The main thread is idle 55–75% of the time.
   - The governor flags `scaleFutile` as true on 13 of 16 legs, so lowering resolution bought nothing.
2. **Once the census-only costs are taken out, GLX has the lightest main thread of the four backends.**
   - Main thread busy per frame: **11.4–25.6 ms on GLX**, against 17–34 ms on three/WebGL2.
   - Callback median: **about 1.4–2.4 ms on GLX**, against 3.7–4.7 ms on WGX and 6–8 ms on three/WebGL2.
3. **Dusk and "lamps by day" each cost about +3.5–4 ms of GLX GPU time over montreal day at equal scale.** The cause is that dusk runs the union of two feature sets:
   - the whole day post set: SSAO, contact shadows, sun god rays with two blur pairs, and SSR;
   - a 40-lamp global loop on every lit fragment.

   Lamp shadow maps and the lamp god-ray march do not run at dusk. Both are gated off when the sun's brightness is about 0.9.
4. **The clearest player-side WGX item is G5 parity.** WGX's night lamp shadow map ignores the lamp's reach, so it rasterises 3.9× the instances GLX does: 2076 against 531 per rebuild at vegas night.

## Ranked findings

### Census instruments
These come first because they decide how every other GLX/WGX number is read.

**I1 — Every GLX census screenshot after the first is the same stale soft-blit frame. The occlusion and multiDraw pixel verdicts on GLX are therefore empty, and so is GLX's `meanLuma`. (S, tools / headless path, CONFIRMED)**

*Mechanism*
- Under a HeadlessChrome UA, GLX sets `preserveDrawingBuffer: true` and creates an opaque `#game-soft` overlay (`glx.js:689-706`).
- It blits to that overlay only when a capture is waiting or after `snapCam`/`park` (`glx.js:563-569`, `:627-629`, `:2873`). Once revealed, the overlay stays at opacity 1 over the live canvas.

*Evidence*
- The overlay text in all 6 censuses (450–455) reads `softPresent {"gen":1}` at the final shot. Exactly one blit ever happened.
- The A/B harness takes `page.screenshot` with no invalidate or await (`gpu-game-check.mjs:1001`).
- The shot gate (`:1117-1126`) only checks that some pixel is non-zero.
- So the "off", "off2" and "on" captures are the same 3D frame. Their 0.03–0.52% "differing pixels" is the live HUD DOM (the TIME text changes between captures).

*Fix*
- Either call `GLX.invalidateSoftPresent()` followed by `await GLX.awaitSoftPresent(8000)` before every census screenshot,
- or better, let GLX honour the SCREENSHOTS native pin (`apex26.wgxCapture=0`): no `preserveDrawingBuffer`, no overlay. three/WebGL2 already screenshots the native canvas on this runner (`meanLuma` 38–42).

*Evidence to capture:* the overlay `gen` increases with every shot, and the off/off2 floor drops to the HUD-only area.

**I2 — The census forces `getError` on every GLX present: 4.9–10.0 ms per frame of main-thread wall time that players do not pay. (S, tools, CONFIRMED)**
- The census sets `glErrDrain=1` (`gpu-game-check.mjs:450`), and GLX drains on every present (`glx.js:72`, `:2875`).
- That is 1364–2889 ms per window: 17–27% of all busy time, and most of the GLX callback median.
- GL error flags are sticky until read (MDN), so one explicit drain per beat (about 1 Hz) loses no error for the `gpuErrors > 0` gate. The only thing lost is a count that was already capped at 8 per drain.
- **Available now, with no code change:** `--ls apex26.glErrDrain=0` overrides the census default, because the `ls` loop runs after the defaults (`:452-455`).
  - The cost: `gpuErrors` then covers only the first 30 presents. Use it for perf runs only.

**I3 — The census WGX leg is not on the player's present path. (S, workflow, CONFIRMED)**
- `gpu-census.yml:349-359` does not pass `--ls apex26.wgxCapture=0`; both three legs do.
- WGX therefore soft-presents every frame (`wgx.js:676`, `:2043-2133`, `wgxSoftPresent: true` on every leg). Each frame it:
  - copies the frame into a staging buffer (`copyTextureToBuffer`);
  - creates a new 2.4–3.7 MB `MAP_READ` buffer (`createBuffer` 0.57–0.96 per frame; `mapAsync` 0.40–0.72 per frame);
  - runs 0.5–1.1 ms of JavaScript in the `wgx.js:2080` callback;
  - calls `putImageData`.
- The HeadlessChrome UA also forces MSAA 1 through `_softAdapter` (`wgx.js:530-537`), which `wgxCapture=0` does not undo.
- The workflow's own comment calls the CPU blit "the 'WebGPU is laggy' class".
- **Available now:** add `apex26.wgxCapture=0` to `CENSUS_LS`. three/WebGPU already gets non-black screenshots with it on this runner.

**I4 — Do not read the governor's end state; read the window.**
- The end snapshot (tier, scale, fps) is a 2048-frame ring that includes the frozen-sim A/B phases.
- The A/B runs on WGX and three legs although the features exist only on GLX (`stats {}`, `gpuTimer` null). That is about 2 × (3 captures + 40 interleaved samples) of wasted time per leg, and those frames enter the ring.
- *Fix:* skip `abFor` when the feature reports `supported: false`. (S, tools.)
- **Spa dusk in the window:** GLX 14.5 fps, WGX **20.8**, three/WebGL2 18.9, three/WebGPU 18.8.
  - WGX was faster at spa dusk than at montreal day (15.4).
  - three/WebGL2's "42.4 fps, tier 0" is the A/B-polluted ring, as hunter A found.

**I5 — The two backends' GPU timers cover different spans. (S)**
- WGX's `gpuMs` times only the lit scene pass: `timestampWrites` sit on `litPass` (`wgx.js:3650-3654`).
- GLX's covers main plus post.
- For target (1), a per-pass timer mode would close this gap:
  - GLX: sequential TIME_ELAPSED queries around the sun, lamp, car, env, mirror, main and post groups;
  - WGX: begin on the first pass and end on the final one.

**I6 — The debug overlay mislabels WGX's present path. (S, diagnostic correctness)**
- It prints "frame: native swapchain (no 2D readback)" while WGX is soft-presenting onto `#game` (`gfx-debug-overlay.js:99-102` checks only for `#game-soft`).
- It should check `GLX.softPresent()` first.

**I7 — GLX's `#game-soft` 2D context is created without `willReadFrequently` (`glx.js:546`).**
- The overlay reads it back on every rebuild: 0.35–1.22 ms per frame of `getImageData` + `pushFrameState` on GLX legs, plus the console warning seen in census454.
- TLX passes the flag (`tlx.js:571`). (S, headless only.)

**P2 — The WGX mirror inset is drawn in a second render pass that reloads the whole drawable. This is the WGX twin of hunt-2 G12. (S, identical, CONFIRMED by code)**

**P3 — Deferring the hi-res player livery causes a single 45–100 ms main-thread block mid-race on both backends. (M, identical final texels, CONFIRMED attribution)**

**P7 — Half of the Uint16 chunk ranges in GLX's road and terrain start at a byte offset that is not a multiple of 4. (S, low, identical)**
- Count on montreal: road 43 of 85, terrain 92 of 180.

  - Memory cost: +232 + 235 KB.
  - Time cost: a first-draw conversion, done by GPU compute when a render pass is open.
- Site: `chunked.js:319-355`.
- *Fix:* pad the offset to an even index with one degenerate triangle (0,0,0) between buckets, or use Uint32 for chunked meshes. A zero-area triangle draws no pixels; prove it with a frozen GLX A/B at MAD 0.000.

### Deferred designs

- **D1 — WGX ULTRA MSAA depth.** It is stored (`wgx.js:3649`) only so the depth-resolve pass can read it back (`:4395-4410`). That is a 33 MB store plus a 33 MB read per frame at 1080p. WebGPU core has no depth resolve attachment; resolving into an R32F colour attachment averages edges, which is a look change. GLX's `blitFramebuffer` depth resolve probably pays the same under ANGLE (unverified).
- **D2 — GLX lamp array as a UBO.** ANGLE-Metal re-uploads the whole fragment default-uniform block on any dirty member (`ProgramExecutableMtl.mm` `commitUniforms`). LIT_FS's block is about 4.6 KB (`c-ublock.cjs`), above the inline threshold, so each draw with a changed material scalar memcpys 4.6 KB in the GPU process. That is ≤ ~1.4 MB per frame and roughly 0.1–0.3 ms of GPU process (arithmetic). Moving `uLight[192]` into a UBO and binding it with `bindBufferRange` leaves about 1.5 KB. M; the GPU-process share is unmeasured.
- **D3 — The GLX static/cars lamp-map split (hunt-2 G5 M part) has not landed.** Vegas night rebuilds 1.0 times per frame at tier 0. A depth blit plus the cars would remove the props redraw. Already listed in hunt 2.
