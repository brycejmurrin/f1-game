# Live input latency and main-thread jank on the phone profile (B·H11, 2026-10-20)

Lane: Fleet B perf hunter B·H11. The ask (Bryce, 2026-10-20): the live site "feels a little laggy" on a phone
(844×390 landscape, touch). This note ranks the main-thread lag sources by measured ms and records the fix.

## Method

All runs: this container's Chromium, SwiftShader, context 844×390 at DPR 2 with `isMobile` and `hasTouch`,
`steerMode` touch and AUTO throttle, Monza, `race()` + `go()`. CPU ×4 (`Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`) is the
phone stand-in. `__apex.headless(true)` skips `render()`: on SwiftShader the draw costs ~12 s a frame and drowns
everything else, and the renderer is B·H10's lane. What remains is input, physics, AI, audio, HUD, style and
layout: the main-thread work a phone pays on top of its GPU.

Scripts (scratch, not committed): `scratch/h11/probe.mjs` (spans, LoAF, event timing, trace with
invalidation tracking, real CDP touch drags), `cpu.mjs` (V8 sampling profile), `episodes.mjs` (slowest
`updateHud` calls with child breakdown), `fitlog.mjs` (every full HUD fit and the key part that changed;
`hud.js` served instrumented through `page.route`), `style-ab.mjs` (CSSOM rule deletion A/B, trace-measured
`UpdateLayoutTree` per wall-second and per frame).

## Ranked lag sources (CPU ×4, ms per frame unless stated)

| # | source | measured | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Style recalc driven by one root `:has()` rule. Every gap-chip text write re-runs `:has()` invalidation on `<html>`/`<body>` and restyles ~425 elements, about 1.4 times a frame | 20.4–20.9 ms/f base; 2.3–7.3 with the rule deleted; 6.2–6.6 with all 106 `:has()` rules deleted | `css/hud.css:222` `.hud-gaps:not(:has(> div:not(:empty)))`, fed by `hText` at `js/ui/hud.js:2279-2280` |
| 2 | HUD 10 Hz tick forced layouts: tick frames p50 4.7, p90 43, max 234 ms | full fit 30–116 ms (start of race; ×1 steady state: one every ~3 s); announce lane `laneRect` 10–39 ms; `phoneFitStampSync` 50–60 ms | `js/ui/hud.js` `fitHud` :1014, `announceLane` :620, `phoneFitStampSync` :937 |
| 3 | Physics + AI catch-up (fixed 1/60 s steps, ≤ 5 a frame) | `update` 42.8 ms/f, of which `updateCar` 32.6 | `js/game.js:4347`, `:4696` (physics lane, not changed here) |
| 4 | `hud-layout` readout clearance re-run from a ResizeObserver and the pack follow-ups | `clearReadouts` 3.2 ms/f across callers | `js/ui/hud-layout.js:764`, observer :883 |
| 5 | Input read path: `remoteBrake`/`remoteThrottle`/`lookingBack` ask `UiLayers.navOpen()` before the cheap `remoteActive()` test, every physics step, with no phone paired | 2.2 ms/f inclusive | `js/input/input.js:1756-1757`, `:1864` |

Not lag sources, measured or read:
- Touch handlers: `onTouchStart` 6–13 ms once at touch-down at ×4, moves well under 1 ms. The window touch
  listeners are passive, the canvas ones must `preventDefault`, and coalesced pointer samples are already read.
- Touch input reaches physics in the same frame: Chrome aligns `touchmove` to rAF, and `Input.steer(stepDt)`
  reads the latest finger position at the next physics step. Render interpolation shows the pose up to one
  1/60 s step behind (`renderAlpha`). That delay is part of the fixed-step design.
- Default BUTTONS steering ramps to full lock in 250 ms (`KEY_RAMP_IN` 4/s). That is a feel setting (STEER
  RATE / SPEED STEER sliders), not latency, and it is left alone.

## Why a text write restyles the page (mechanism, cited)

- Blink: `CharacterData::DidModifyData` reports `kTextChanged` to the parent; `Element::ChildrenChanged` always runs
  `CheckForEmptyStyleChange`, which never compares old and new text, so "12" over "11" in a lone text child still
  fires `PseudoStateChanged(:empty)`. A selector with `:empty` inside `:has()` then walks
  `InvalidateAncestorsOrSiblingsAffectedByHas` up to `<html>`. That is why the `hText` `.data` fast path
  (PERF-P2-HTEXT-2026-10-18) did not help the gap chips, which sit under `.hud-gaps:not(:has(> div:not(:empty)))`.
  Sources: chromium `main` `core/dom/character_data.cc`, `core/dom/element.cc`, `core/css/style_engine.cc`;
  https://blogs.igalia.com/blee/posts/2023/05/31/how-blink-invalidates-styles-when-has-in-use.html
- WebKit `main` skips this for non-empty→non-empty text since bug 315655 (314037@main, 2026-05-28,
  https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=315655); shipped Safari releases before it used the coarser path, and
  `:has(:empty)` had stale-state bugs (https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=286403). Removing the rule is the
  fix on both engines.
- For the follow-up (#2/#4): reads before any write in a frame are free, each read after a write forces style and
  layout (https://gist.github.com/paulirish/5d52fb081b3570c81e3a,
  https://web.dev/articles/avoid-large-complex-layouts-and-layout-thrashing); ResizeObserver callbacks run inside
  the rendering steps, so a callback that writes re-runs style and layout in the same frame
  (https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/webappapis.html#event-loop-processing-model). Inherited custom properties
  written on `<html>` restyle the document; registered `inherits: false` ones are ~850x cheaper in Blink
  (https://web.dev/blog/at-property-performance).

## Fix (PR #3245)

1. `css/hud.css`: `.hud-gaps[data-empty] { display: none; }` replaces `.hud-gaps:not(:has(> div:not(:empty)))`.
   `js/ui/hud.js` `gapsEmptySync()` writes `data-empty` only when both lines go empty or one gets text; the plate
   starts `data-empty` in `index.html`. Same collapse, no root invalidation.
2. `js/input/input.js`: `remoteThrottle`, `remoteBrake` and `lookingBack` test `remoteActive()` (a clock compare)
   before `remoteDrivingBlocked()` (the UI-layer walk). Pure `&&` reorder of side-effect-free terms: same result.

**Measured** (`style-ab.mjs monza 6 4 3 before,after`, `before` = HEAD~2's `hud.css`/`hud.js`/`input.js` served
by `page.route`, the old rule confirmed present in every before arm and absent in every after arm; 6 s trace
windows at CPU x4, interleaved):

| arm | style recalc ms/frame | style ms per wall-second |
|---|---|---|
| before | 16.33, 19.22, 17.80 (median 17.8) | 223, 160, 154 |
| after | 10.09, 10.28, 10.63 (median 10.3) | 96, 99, 80 |

-42 % style recalculation per frame. Layout is unchanged (1.3-1.8 ms/frame).

## Follow-up (branch cursor/hud-tick-h11b): what remained after #3245

Re-profiled on the post-#3245 tree, same shape and settings (CPU x4, 8 s): 76 → 119 frames, `updateHud`
15.2 → 9.5 ms/frame, HUD tick p90 43 → 18.9 ms, `remoteDrivingBlocked` gone from the profile. The tail is the
full fit: 75–145 ms episodes at x4. `clearReadouts` (2.6 ms/frame) is mostly downstream of it: in 10 s, 12 of its
20 calls came from `packPhone` (the fit and its double-rAF + 120 ms follow-ups), 4 from the fit, and only 4 from
the readout ResizeObserver (the radio card resizing).

1. **The 3 s same-key backstop re-fits only what resized.** With the fit key unchanged, `fitHud` still ran a full
   fit every 30 ticks. A ResizeObserver over `#hud > *`, `#hud-dock > *`, `.dock > *` and `[data-hl]` reports
   size changes without a layout read. The backstop re-fits when one of them resized since the last fit settled.
   Sizes that change within `FIT_SETTLE_MS` (400 ms) of a full fit come from the fit's own zoom writes and its
   pack follow-ups, so they become the baseline. Every 10th backstop (~30 s) still re-fits unconditionally. Key
   changes, clash re-opens and forced refreshes are untouched. Without ResizeObserver (mini-dom VMs, old engines)
   it behaves as before. Measured (`fitlog.mjs`, Monza, 30 s at x4, two interleaved reps): 11 → 8 full fits.
   The remaining same-key re-fits are real resizes (the DAMAGE chip appearing or widening, the LIMITS chip
   appearing). Pinned by `tests/unit/hud-pins.test.mjs` "the 3 s same-key backstop…", which fails against the
   old `hud.js`.
2. **`touchRangePx` caches the viewport's long edge until `resize`.** It read `innerWidth`/`innerHeight` on every
   `touchmove` (via `touchCmd` and `updateDragAnchor`); the trace showed Layout and UpdateLayoutTree under it in
   the touch handler. Pinned by `tests/unit/touch-drag-anchor.test.mjs` "the drag range reads the viewport on
   resize…", which fails against the old `input.js`.
