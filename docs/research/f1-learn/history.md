# How Formula 1’s rules evolved

The rulebook shapes what teams build, how they race, what they can spend, and how much development they can do. These levers sometimes move independently. F1 introduced the team cost cap and a development handicap in 2021, then switched to a new car design in 2022 after postponing that change during the pandemic. Use the season and issue number when reconstructing a rule or a disputed result. ## The major resets

| Era | What changed and why it mattered |
| --- | --- |
| **1950–1960** | The Drivers’ World Championship began in 1950. Most championship Grands Prix in 1952–53 ran to Formula Two rules, and F1 introduced a 2.5-litre naturally aspirated formula in 1954. The Constructors’ championship began in 1958. Early drivers’ scoring could count only a subset of results, so reconstructing historical standings requires the original scoring rules. [F1 championship origins](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/why-is-it-called-formula-1-and-12-other-questions-about-the-championships.1GHeel6u4jga6hMpX2eFs1.1GHeel6u4jga6hMpX2eFs1) · [F1 points history](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/how-many-world-championship-titles-would-f1-drivers-have-won-using-the.182RC42vohx8STIpJR5o26) |
| **1961–1966** | The naturally aspirated limit fell from 2.5 to 1.5 litres in 1961 and a 450 kg minimum arrived. Ferrari’s 156 was ready for the switch, and Phil Hill and Ferrari won the championships. In 1966 the naturally aspirated limit doubled to 3.0 litres, with 1.5 litres allowed with forced induction. Jack Brabham won in his own team’s Repco-engined car while several rivals struggled with engines for the new formula. [F1 regulation resets](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/the-key-regulation-changes-in-f1-history-and-the-teams-that-nailed-them.2iq8c5E6S1HOffT5PBLl6i) · [F1 Jack Brabham biography](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/drivers-hall-of-fame-jack-brabham.3XK2XWdL0moCmnSC55m84J) |
| **Late 1960s–1983** | Wings appeared in 1968. Failures of high, suspension-mounted wings in 1969 prompted restrictions on how aerodynamic devices could be mounted. Lotus’s wing-shaped underbody and side skirts made ground effect decisive in the late 1970s. The 1983 flat-bottom rule curtailed that underfloor design, pushing teams back toward wing-generated downforce. [F1 on wing cars and flat bottoms](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/re-writing-the-f1-rule-book-part-1-from-wing-cars-to-flat-bottoms.EJZ73Lnn0c6EgcMKMAWOU) · [F1 on the Lotus 79](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/tech-tuesday-the-lotus-79-f1s-ground-effect-marvel.sAD9PXt7mC8iMSwwe6CCw) |
| **1977–1989** | Renault exploited the 1.5-litre forced-induction option in 1977. Turbos went from an unreliable alternative to the dominant architecture. Fuel and boost limits constrained them toward the end of the era, including a 2.5-bar and 150-litre limit for turbos in 1988. From 1989 the formula required naturally aspirated engines of up to 3.5 litres. McLaren-Honda won in both 1988 and 1989, changing from a turbo V6 to a naturally aspirated V10 between seasons. [F1 regulation resets](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/the-key-regulation-changes-in-f1-history-and-the-teams-that-nailed-them.2iq8c5E6S1HOffT5PBLl6i) · [Honda on the 1988 turbo limits](https://global.honda/en/POWEREDbyHONDA/1988_ra168e/) |
| **1994–2005** | Active suspension and traction control were banned for 1994, and race refuelling returned. Separate safety changes followed the deaths of Roland Ratzenberger and Ayrton Senna at Imola, including measures affecting aerodynamics and circuits. The engine displacement limit fell from 3.5 to 3.0 litres in 1995. For 1998, maximum car width fell from 2,000 to 1,800 mm and dry tyres gained grooves to reduce mechanical grip. The 2005 requirement to use a single set of dry tyres through qualifying and the race changed race strategy and was reversed for 2006. [F1 on driver aids and safety](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/re-writing-the-f1-rulebook-part-2--from-driver-aids-to-stepped.6ng1HQEGGsImQKekgaG4sw) · [F1 on narrow cars and grooved tyres](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/re-writing-the-f1-rulebook-part-3-narrow-cars-find-their-groove.5YeEtrJfLako8UUG6Smgo4) · [FIA Imola safety timeline](https://www.fia.com/news/legacy-safety-how-fia-responded-tragedy-imola-1994-and-launched-drive-safer-racing) |
| **2006–2013** | A 2.4-litre V8 replaced the 3.0-litre V10 as the core engine formula in 2006. The 2009 rules restored slicks, reduced and reshaped external aerodynamics, and permitted KERS to recover and redeploy braking energy. The FIA’s International Court of Appeal upheld the double-diffuser designs challenged at the start of 2009. Race refuelling ended for 2010, shifting fuel-load management to the car and tyres across a full race. DRS arrived in 2011 for controlled drag reduction behind another car. [F1 on the 2009 reset](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/re-writing-the-f1rulebook-part-4-cleaner-cars-kers-and-return-ofslicks.1NUrjfG83a6aE0QSuAkGK8) · [FIA double-diffuser appeal decision](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/judgements/ICA-14-04-2009-a.pdf) · [F1 refuelling history](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/f1-quest-perfect-pit-stop.1lwNTV7z6MiEA2weCMsS8E) |
| **2014–2021** | The turbo-hybrid era began with 1.6-litre V6s, an MGU-K that recovers energy under braking, and an MGU-H linked to the turbo. Fuel-flow and total race-fuel limits made efficiency part of performance. The 2017 chassis and tyre rules widened the cars and tyres and allowed more downforce. The Halo arrived in 2018. In 2021 the team cost cap and sliding-scale aerodynamic testing took effect before the 2022 chassis reset. [F1 regulation resets](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/the-key-regulation-changes-in-f1-history-and-the-teams-that-nailed-them.2iq8c5E6S1HOffT5PBLl6i) · [FIA safety timeline](https://www.fia.com/news/legacy-safety-how-fia-responded-tragedy-imola-1994-and-launched-drive-safer-racing) · [F1’s 2021 rule changes](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/aero-tweaks-new-tyres-and-the-cost-cap-the-2021-f1-rules-and-regulation.r1cq36aI6bvzMeZtiFru3) |
| **2022–2025** | Shaped underfloor tunnels and diffuser geometry concentrated more aerodynamic work under the car. The wake-management goal was to let drivers follow through corners with less downforce loss. Eighteen-inch wheels replaced 13-inch wheels. After cars experienced severe vertical oscillation, the FIA introduced a metric for monitoring it and later changed floor and diffuser geometry requirements. The FIA also reported that the initial wake improvement declined as teams developed their cars through the cycle. [F1 on 2022 car development](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/in-depth-brave-new-world-how-the-f1-car-evolved-into-2022s-beast.3B7n3Bjs4Rduz8CL3cBWMf) · [FIA 2022–23 floor updates](https://www.fia.com/news/world-motor-sport-council-approves-f1-2026-power-unit-regulations-and-updated-2022-2023) · [FIA on the 2026 changes](https://www.fia.com/news/f1s-new-era-everything-you-need-know-about-how-fia-making-formula-1-more-competitive-more) |
| **2026** | Smaller cars use active front and rear wing elements to manage drag. Overtake Mode uses electrical energy allocation to help an eligible following driver, replacing DRS’s role as the designated overtaking aid. The 1.6-litre turbo V6 remains, the MGU-H is removed, MGU-K maximum power rises to 350 kW, and the fuel must meet the FIA’s advanced sustainable fuel requirements. Eligibility and energy limits depend on the applicable regulations and event parameters. [FIA 2026 overview](https://www.fia.com/news/f1s-new-era-everything-you-need-know-about-how-fia-making-formula-1-more-competitive-more) · [FIA 2026 rulebook and revisions](https://www.fia.com/regulation/category/2182) |

Sporting formats have their own timeline. A single qualifying lap under the 2003 rules gave way to the three knockout sessions introduced in 2006. Sprint arrived in 2021 and originally helped set the Grand Prix grid. It became a standalone points race with separate qualifying in 2023. The fastest-lap bonus returned in 2019 for a top-ten finisher and was removed for 2025. Comparing championship outcomes requires the scoring and weekend formats in force that year. ## Safety and race control

- Jackie Stewart’s crash at Spa in 1966 made him a prominent advocate for seat belts, barriers and properly staffed medical facilities. In 1981 McLaren introduced a carbon-fibre monocoque with the MP4/1, an example of a team innovation that became foundational to later crash-survival standards. [F1’s Stewart biography](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/drivers-hall-of-fame-jackie-stewart.18715uErEG7LS83UDkeqP4) · [F1 safety milestones](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/5-crucial-milestone-moments-in-f1-safety-technology.4rlVLaoq24DjmJKtusZ0tp)
- After Imola in 1994 the FIA changed several circuits and barriers, then strengthened crash protection and medical readiness. Subsequent measures included taller cockpit sides and cockpit-rim static load tests in 1996, wheel tethers in 1999, mandatory HANS in 2003, visor reinforcement in 2011 after Felipe Massa’s 2009 injury, and mandatory Halo and biometric gloves in 2018. Each layer addresses a different failure mode, including flying wheels, head acceleration, cockpit intrusion and delayed medical assessment. [FIA safety timeline](https://www.fia.com/news/legacy-safety-how-fia-responded-tragedy-imola-1994-and-launched-drive-safer-racing)
- Jules Bianchi struck a recovery vehicle during the 2014 Japanese GP. The FIA accident panel examined the interaction of track conditions, speed and recovery procedures. The FIA introduced the Virtual Safety Car in 2015 to enforce reduced speeds using a time delta around the circuit. A full Safety Car physically collects the field, while a VSC slows drivers without the same bunching effect. [FIA accident panel and VSC approval](https://www.fia.com/news/world-motor-sport-council-2014-doha)
- In Romain Grosjean’s 2020 Bahrain crash, the FIA found that the Halo, survival cell, HANS and other restraints protected the driver’s survival space. The FIA investigated damage to the fuel system, barrier performance and the response, then identified improvements to fuel-cell installations, barriers, fire protection and medical equipment. [FIA Grosjean investigation](https://www.fia.com/news/fia-concludes-investigation-romain-grosjeans-accident-2020-bahrain-formula-1-grand-prix-and)
- The FIA’s 2021 Abu Dhabi review found human error in the process that allowed only some lapped cars to pass the Safety Car and found that the car was called in without completing the additional lap required by the procedure. The result remained final. The FIA subsequently clarified the wording, changed the lapped-car identification process, reorganized race control support and added a Remote Operations Centre. [FIA Abu Dhabi review and WMSC decisions](https://api.fia.com/news/fia-announces-world-motor-sport-council-decisions-25)

## Who writes, applies and interprets the rules

The FIA is the regulator and Formula 1 is the commercial rights holder. The original Concorde Agreement in 1981 formalized how the governing body, commercial interests and entrants would work together. For the 2026 cycle the parties announced commercial and governance agreements separately. The commercial agreement covers the economic arrangements among Formula 1 and the teams. The governance agreement was confirmed by Formula 1, the FIA and all 11 teams and underpins the structure through 2030. The public announcements describe the agreements’ purpose, with detailed provisions governed by the agreements themselves. [F1 on Concorde’s origins](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/analysis-what-the-new-concorde-agreement-means-for-formula-1.1Z97Z6vRwfDTHVhGbSS510) · [2026 commercial agreement announcement](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/f1-confirms-2026-concorde-commercial-agreement-signed-by-all-teams.5WM4jliEP1Z3C4WPP2V0eJ.html) · [2026 governance agreement announcement](https://corp.formula1.com/formula-1-the-fia-and-all-eleven-teams-confirm-signing-of-2026-concorde-governance-agreement/)

The F1 Commission considers changes, and the FIA World Motor Sport Council approves regulations. The FIA International Sporting Code provides the wider legal framework. At an event, Race Control runs sessions and issues operational instructions, the technical delegate reports compliance findings, and stewards adjudicate alleged breaches and protests. Published driving standards and penalty guidelines help explain how stewards apply judgment to the facts of an incident. The Code provides routes for a right of review when the evidentiary requirements are met, and appeals go through the specified process where a decision is appealable. The 2009 double-diffuser judgment is a concrete example of a challenge moving from event stewards to the FIA International Court of Appeal. [FIA International Sporting Code](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/2026_international_sporting_code_fr-en_marked_up_version_v4_16.12.2025.pdf) · [FIA driving standards guidelines](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/2026_f1_driving_standards_guidelines.pdf) · [FIA 2009 appeal judgment](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/judgements/ICA-14-04-2009-a.pdf)

In 2026 the published F1 rules are organized into **A** general provisions, **B** sporting, **C** technical, **D** team financial, **E** power unit manufacturer financial and **F** operational. For November 2026, the current issues listed by the FIA are A03, B09, C21, D08, E07 and F11, all published on 19 October. Early announcement articles describe the design intent. The current articles, appendices and event-specific FIA documents should control a decision involving a particular race or test. ## Financial and operational limits

- **Team cost cap.** The 2021 base cap was US$145 million for a 21-race season, with adjustments. The announced baseline fell to US$140 million in 2022 and US$135 million for 2023–25, subject to the applicable adjustments and later revisions. Cap accounting uses defined Relevant Costs, exclusions and adjustments, with financial reporting and regulatory review. Driver pay, specified highly paid personnel and marketing are examples of exclusions whose details belong in the applicable edition. Under 2026 D4.1.2 the base is US$215 million for up to 24 competitions, plus US$1.8 million per competition beyond 24, subject to indexation. Changes in accounting scope make the headline amounts poor proxies for changes in like-for-like spending. [FIA introduction of cap](https://api.fia.com/news/world-motor-sport-council-approves-changes-2020-2021-and-2022-fia-formula-1-regulations) · [F1 on 2021 cap exclusions](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/the-2021-f1-cost-cap-explained-what-has-changed-and-why.5O1Te8udKLmkUl4PyVZtUJ) · [FIA Section D Issue 08](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_d_financial_-_f1_teams_-_iss_08_-_2026-10-01.pdf)
- **Power unit manufacturer cap.** Section E applies to the manufacturer’s own regulated activities. E2.3.1 in Issue 07 sets an indexed US$148.5 million in each of the three full reporting years before a manufacturer’s inaugural season and indexed US$190 million from its inaugural season onward. The reporting basis, adjustments and exclusions differ from the team rules. [FIA Section E Issue 07](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_e_financial_-_pu_manufacturers_-_iss_07_-_2026-10-19.pdf)
- **Cost-cap enforcement.** Red Bull’s 2021 Accepted Breach Agreement required a US$7 million fine and a 10% reduction of its applicable aerodynamic testing coefficient for 12 months. The FIA recorded a £1,864,000 overspend and acknowledged it would have been £432,652 (0.37%) with the correct treatment of a notional tax credit. The case illustrates how cost accounting can produce a sanction against development capacity. In the same review cycle the FIA classified Aston Martin’s breach as procedural. [FIA Red Bull breach agreement](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/rbr_public_summary_aba_-_article_6.32_1.pdf) · [FIA results of 2021 cap review](https://api.fia.com/news/fia-completes-review-under-2021-fia-formula-1-financial-regulations)
- **Development time.** The 2021 sliding scale grants smaller wind-tunnel and CFD allocations to teams higher in the Constructors’ standings. In 2026, F4.6 assigns 70% of reference allowances to first, 100% to seventh and 115% to tenth or lower and new teams. The system measures wind-tunnel runs, wind-on hours, occupancy and CFD resources across defined testing periods. Section F also defines factory shutdowns and power-unit test-bench restrictions. Track mileage is separately regulated; for example, 2025 introduced a 1,000 km limit over four days for a team’s championship race drivers in Testing of Previous Cars. Additional allowance and additional spending are distinct resources.
