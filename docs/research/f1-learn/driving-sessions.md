# F1 Learn driving labs

Two guided sessions use repeatable scenarios to connect a driving choice to an observable effect. Each takes about 10 minutes with optional Apex 26 attempts. [Physics equations and sources](physics.md), [Apex 26 integration research](game.md)

## Shared session flow

| State | Learner sees and does | Session behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Predict | A corner or stint card with one question and two or three choices | Record the prediction for the debrief. Let the learner move on after an incorrect guess. |
| Explore | One variable unlocked at a time, with synchronized track and plot cursors | Recalculate on input. Announce the result after adjustment, and provide a text equivalent of each plot marker. |
| Compare | Two pinned runs with a common scale and matching start conditions | Keep the baseline visible. Show the changed variable and independently display the outcome. |
| Apply | A new case with fewer cues, a reset, and an optional hint | Score the relationship the learner demonstrates. Retain the last attempt through review and reset to the original seed on retry. |
| Debrief | Prediction, choice, outcome and a short explanation | Mark the session complete after the learner reviews the comparison and solves the transfer case. Offer free exploration and, if available, a separate Apex 26 challenge. |

Keep simulation inputs in SI and display speed in km/h, longitudinal and lateral acceleration in g, temperatures in °C and distances in m. Round for display after calculating. Label each trace **Lab model** or **Apex 26 run**. Provide named presets, keyboard-adjustable inputs and a textual summary in addition to colors. On an 844×390 touch view, put the current control below the plot and let the learner switch between plots while retaining the comparison.

## Lab 1. Spend grip through a braking zone

**Outcome.** The learner can explain why braking and turning compete for tyre force, change the brake release for an entry, and distinguish brake demand, axle load and a lock cue.

**Scene.** One flat approach and constant-radius bend. Start with an 800 kg illustrative car, 3.5 m wheelbase, 0.30 m CG height, 47% static front load, constant surface grip and no aero. Freeze the entry when the learner compares demand. In the combined-force view, express braking and lateral demands as fractions of their respective peak tyre-axis capacities. Each control runs from 0 to 100% of its own pure-axis peak. The force-ellipse overlay is a scale model of capacity; actual slip-angle and slip-ratio response requires measured tyre characterization. [Brach & Brach, SAE force ellipse study](https://doi.org/10.4271/2011-01-0094), [Pacejka & Bakker, Magic Formula](https://doi.org/10.1080/00423119208969994)

| Beat | Prompt and action | Reveal and success check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight-line baseline | “At the same entry speed, how much space does a weaker stop need?” Pin 200→100 km/h at 10 m/s². Select 7 m/s² with the same speeds. | Constant-deceleration distances are 115.7 m and 165.3 m. The weaker stop starts 49.6 m earlier to reach the same target at the same point. Both distances ignore driver reaction and use modeled constant deceleration. |
| Combine braking and steering | “Can this entry sustain 80% braking and 80% turning together?” Drag the normalized demand point from (0.80, 0.00) to (0.80, 0.80). | Requested utilization is 113.1%. The displayed radial projection reaches (0.707, 0.707). At a fixed lateral demand of 0.80, the achievable braking ceiling is 0.60. Show both constrained solutions only when their respective controls are active. |
| Practice brake release | Scrub approach → turn-in → apex. Steering demand rises from 0.00 to 0.60 to 0.90. Start with a pinned brake trace of 0.95 at all three markers. Move the turn-in and apex brake controls. | Accept any sampled trace satisfying utilization ≤1 at all markers. The available brake ceilings are 1.00, 0.80 and 0.436. One successful trace is 0.95 → 0.75 → 0.35. Display “Grip margin restored at entry.” A corner animation with interpolation should check the whole path if its interpolated demands depart from straight lines in the force plane. This exercise illustrates demand feasibility at the chosen speeds. |
| Add load and brake bias | At a frozen requested 1 g stop, show both axle loads and the brake-pressure split. Predict which end reaches its limit first at 50% front, then change bias. | With 9.81 m/s² and no aero, front load rises from 3688.6 N to 4361.2 N, rear falls from 4159.4 N to 3486.8 N. With μ=1, the demanded 7848 N exactly equals the total capacity. At equal bias, the rear receives 3924 N, 437.2 N over its peak. The instantaneous capacity-matched front fraction is 55.57%. Probe 60% and see the front exceed its peak. |
| Transfer check | “At a lateral demand of 0.60, keep as much braking as possible while leaving the request inside the envelope.” Allow brake changes only, with an optional square-root hint. | Accept 0.78–0.80 on a hundredth-step control. Above 0.80, display the numerical amount over budget. Below 0.78, show the remaining brake margin and let the learner retry. |

Treat the bias case as a force-demand snapshot with prescribed deceleration, before the car reacts. A sustained rear overload changes actual deceleration and then changes the loads. Brake bias sets the brake contribution requested at each axle. The longitudinal moment and acceleration set the load transfer. At a different speed, aero and axle lateral demand can alter the best balance. Use “excess demand” for the snapshot. A wheel lock requires wheel angular speed to reach zero while the car keeps moving. In a 2026 hybrid sidebar, show the rear request split across hydraulic and regenerative braking. [MIT Formula SAE load-transfer analysis](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/139209/gaither-gaither-sb-2-2021-thesis.pdf), [Brembo on Formula 1 rear brake-by-wire](https://www.brembo.com/en/motorsport/formula1)

**Debrief responses.** If the learner expects more steering to force a tighter turn, highlight the rising requested vector and the limited achieved lateral component. If they equate high brake pressure with high deceleration, compare requested and available force on the overloaded axle. If they assume brake release means coasting throughout the bend, replay the feasible trace with some brake remaining at turn-in.

**Apex 26 challenge candidate.** Use a verified clear approach to Bahrain Turn 1 with a fixed car, assists, tyre policy and entry speed. Replay a held-brake and tapered-brake input trace through the same approach. Compare speed and distance at turn-in, lateral deviation, and the documented kinematic readings from `physState()`. Record `axFrac` as a game longitudinal-use signal and `drivenSlip` as the remaining driven-axle circle signal when present. Angle or circle budget cannot establish wheel lock. `player-forces.js` describes its front lockup cue as render and feel only. The first build should report the observed differences. Choose numerical challenge cutoffs only once repeated captured runs confirm a meaningful difference for the fixed scenario. [Game debug hooks](../../DEBUG-HOOKS.md), [Player force implementation](../../../js/physics/player-forces.js)

## Lab 2. Follow the water and temperature

**Outcome.** The learner can match tread to conditions, explain why standing water and speed can combine to remove grip, and use both surface and bulk temperature to choose an action.

**Scene.** One loop with a drying line and an optional standing-water segment. Treat water level as a synthetic slider from 0 to 1, and read it independently of rain intensity. Use preset line/sector values for local demonstrations in the lab. In the game challenge, use the game’s available weather state and expose its distinct telemetry. Pirelli describes Intermediate and Full Wet as distinct tread options. The lab uses its own illustrative values. [Pirelli wet-weather switching discussion](https://www.pirelli.com/global/en-ww/race/racingspot/formula-1/when-it-s-time-to-change-from-slick-to-wet-tyres-in-formula-1-52943/), [Pirelli 2026 tyre range](https://press.pirelli.com/pirelli-reveals-2026-f1-tyres-a-fresh-logo-design-and-new-compounds/)

| Beat | Prompt and action | Reveal and success check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose a line | “Which line would you try here?” Compare a rubbered line (dry μ=1.30, damp μ=0.75) to an off-line strip (dry μ=1.08, damp μ=0.90). Fix radius at 50 m on either line for this first comparison. | In the no-aero corner fixture the dry line permits 90.9 km/h versus 82.9 km/h. The damp line permits 69.0 versus 75.6 km/h. Then make the off-line radius 40 m in the damp. Its modeled limit falls to 67.7 km/h. Real line choice depends on the local water, surface, path geometry, obstacles and competitors. |
| Separate wet grip from aquaplaning | Lock speed at 230 km/h; compare damp `w=0.50` and standing water `w=1.00`. Change tread while retaining speed, then repeat at 180 km/h. | Display base wet friction and an independent aquaplaning multiplier. In the Apex-derived multiplier fixture at 230 km/h in standing water, the slick gives 0.500, intermediate 0.977, full wet 1.000. At 180 km/h the slick rises to 0.837. For `w≤0.72` this particular multiplier stays at 1.000 for all speeds; damp friction can still be lower. |
| Work an out-lap | “When the tyre feels better, which temperature moved first?” Pin both nodes at 70°C, use the scripted 100°C equilibrium segment for nine seconds, then compare. | Surface reaches 89.0°C and bulk 76.8°C. Step into a drying-line versus damp-line repeat with separate equilibrium presets. Show the mid-run sample, trend and the optimum band. |
| Manage a hot intermediate | Start at 105°C surface and 95°C bulk. Pin the dry-line equilibrium at 110°C and select a cool-strip equilibrium at 75°C instead. Compare both traces after nine seconds. | On the cool strip surface falls to 86.0°C and bulk to 90.5°C. On the dry line the two values reach 108.2°C and 98.4°C. A hotter surface can cool quickly while the bulk changes more slowly. Remove the cool-strip option in a standing-water hazard segment, so cooling and grip decisions stay distinct. |
| Transfer check | Give the learner a drying loop with an isolated ponded braking approach. Ask them to choose a tread for the majority conditions, then adjust speed before reaching the pond. In the intermediate preset use `w=1`, initial 250 km/h and the criterion “Keep at least 98% of the aquaplaning multiplier.” | At 250 km/h the intermediate multiplier is 0.875. The threshold is 229 km/h or below. At 225 km/h it is about 0.990. Review the two decisions separately. The criterion applies only to the synthetic loss multiplier and supplies no real-world safe-speed threshold. |

**Numerical models.** For the line experiment use `vLimit = sqrt(μ g R)`. The aquaplaning exercise uses the current Apex 26 standard-speed model `A = 1 − 0.5 D r²`, with `D = clamp((w−0.72)/0.28, 0, 1)` and `r = clamp((vKmh−vOnset)/70, 0, 1)`. Onsets are 140, 215 and 290 km/h respectively for slick, intermediate and full wet in game standard-speed units. A multiplier of 1 indicates that this component applied no grip loss. The underlying wet-road friction and other modifiers still apply. Hydroplaning research gives physical context for speed and water depth. Tread, surface, pressure and water conditions affect the phenomenon. [Apex 26 tyre model](../../../js/physics/tyre-model.js), [Horne & Dreher, NASA TN D-2056](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19640000612), [Horne, effects of depth, texture and tread](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19650033238)

For each thermal node use `T(t) = Teq + (T0 − Teq) exp(−t/τ)` for a fixed segment. Use illustrative τ of 9 s and 35 s for surface and bulk and an example optimum band of 85–95°C. Carry each node’s final value into the next segment. The exercise provides independently lagged channels and omits inter-node coupling. A physical racing tyre model accounts for heat generation, conduction and fluid/track exchange across layers. Do not interpret the synthetic band as a blanket setting or a team target. [Farroni et al., Thermo Racing Tyre](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11012-013-9821-9), [Optimal control with temperature-dependent racing tyre friction](https://doi.org/10.1080/00423114.2011.622406)

**Debrief responses.** If the learner always avoids the rubbered line in the wet, replay the radius change and reveal the surface and speed margin side by side. If they expect the full wet to dominate every mixed lap, compare dry-line pace, tread suitability and temperature across a complete lap. If they want to slide the tyre to warm it faster, show how sliding can heat the surface while using grip and increasing wear, with bulk response lagging. The Apex 26 graining and blistering meters are separate game models. Cooling the surface leaves current wear in place. [Formula 1 wet driving](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/watch-the-challenge-of-wet-weather-driving-and-how-verstappen-and-norris.1D3ZJJlakbWe2nlaFp8cXI), [Apex 26 tyre telemetry](../../DEBUG-HOOKS.md)

**Apex 26 challenge candidates.**

- Wet grip comparison. Recreate a fixed wet or rain scenario with matched fuel state, tread starting age, same assists and route. Use documented `weather()` or `weatherArc()` in development and fit each tread through `tyres({fit})`. Compare actual braking-point and corner-entry traces. Keep the local standing-water and drying-strip overlays in the lab until a matching game fixture can expose local surface state.
- Out-lap comparison. Enable real tyre wear explicitly in the scenario, fit the same fresh set on each restart and compare steady versus slide-heavy inputs. Read `tempS`, `tempB`, `tempOpt`, `tempWindow`, `tempGrip`, `grain`, `blister` and axle wear via `tyres()`. Keep game temperature constants and thresholds out of the lab scoring. Restore prior settings after the scenario exits. Public actions can use the game’s scenario entry point once implemented. Debug hooks support evidence capture. [Apex 26 debug hook reference](../../DEBUG-HOOKS.md)

## Verification checklist

- Compare fixtures against the worked numbers within ±0.1 displayed units for speed, temperature and distance, ±1 N for load, and ±0.001 for force or aqua multipliers. Use unrounded inputs for scoring.
- Verify the force ellipse at both pure-axis limits and at zero. Confirm that increasing lateral demand from 0.60 to 0.80 reduces available brake fraction from 0.80 to 0.60. Confirm that the four illustrative wheel loads sum to total weight in the no-aero load snapshot.
- Verify that the aquaplaning model is continuous on either side of `w=0.72` and each speed onset. Verify that time zero leaves both temperatures unchanged, nine seconds moves the surface more than the bulk for equal initial values, and both approach the chosen equilibrium in a long segment.
- Run each session after an incorrect first prediction and replay after reset to verify reachability. Check keyboard and touch input, that trace comparisons survive chart switching, and that every scored result has a text equivalent.
- In game evidence runs, save the scenario settings and build SHA with each trace. Report a game challenge as unverified until both baseline and comparison have been captured in the intended entry state.
