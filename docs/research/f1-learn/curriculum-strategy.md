# Pillar 4 · Race strategy

Research checked 23 November 2026. These three candidates answer different questions. The pit-window lesson compares routes and queue order within one neutralisation. The undercut lesson compares rival stop sequences over adjacent laps. The fuel and tyre lesson plans the entire weekend and race. Use the current FIA regulations for the general rule, the applicable issue and event notes for historical cases, and labelled model inputs for counterfactuals. ## 12. Can I stop without losing track position?

**Aha.** Relative pit loss is pit-route time minus track-route time between comparable markers. A neutralisation can reduce the difference without speeding up service. VSC generally preserves the field’s gaps approximately, subject to local timing and stops. A physical Safety Car queues the field, so the timing window and the slot available at the exit may tell different stories. A driver can lose places through a discounted stop.

**Interaction.** Extend the existing VSC lesson with a small field and a common track/pit map. Show elapsed time, distance, flag message, queue order and the pit-exit signal on a single replay. Pause before commitment and let the player choose **Box now**, **Wait one lap**, or **Stay out**, then predict the rejoin slot. After revealing that slot, let the player rerun the same call under green, VSC or a physical Safety Car. Move the restart across the pit-entry and pit-exit markers, add an optional double-stack delay, and use the field’s predicted arrivals to reconstruct the order. The model should accommodate a closed exit as a wait before crossing. It should show a closed pit entry as a distinct restriction, with the published rule and its permitted exception visible. [Existing VSC worked lesson](race-sessions.md) · [FIA Section B Issue 09, B1.6.3–4 and B5.12–13](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf)

**Worked challenge.** Keep the existing fictional 400 m sector and 30 s pit route. Green track time 8 s gives 22 s relative loss. Full VSC track time 18 s gives 12 s loss. With a rival 14 s back at the common comparison, the same call rejoins 8 s behind in green or 2 s ahead under VSC. In a late-restart case, 200 m at 80 km/h takes 9 s and 200 m at 180 km/h takes 4 s, so 17 s is lost and the car rejoins 3 s behind. For a physical Safety Car branch, give two other cars independent synthetic arrivals at the merge point at 26 s and 29 s, and the player arrival at 30 s, all in one clock. The queue slots in before the player regardless of the VSC replay’s predicted position. A four-second double-stack or exit delay moves the player’s crossing to 34 s. Hold the other arrivals fixed for that isolated comparison.

**Event evidence and telemetry.** In Melbourne 2026, Mercedes stopped Russell and Antonelli on lap 12 under VSC, and Leclerc/Hamilton stopped on laps 25/28. In Monaco, the physical Safety Car triggered by Stroll’s crash prompted many second stops. Las Vegas adds a timing and judgement example. Verstappen and Russell stopped on lap 19 with recorded pit-sequence durations of 21.220 and 22.090 s. The FIA ruled that Hadjar drove unnecessarily slowly under the Safety Car to create space for a double-stack, impeding following cars, and added 10 seconds. Use that ruling as a bound on what the player can call. The FIA’s Race Control Messages, Lap Chart and Pit Stop Summary establish state/order/timestamp anchors. A pit summary duration alone cannot establish relative track loss or the stationary portion. ## 13. Does stopping first actually get me past?

**Aha.** The undercut succeeds when the early stopper’s usable pace advantage and differences in stop cost recover the pre-stop gap by the time both cars have stopped. Fresh tyres bring potential grip. Warm-up and the car ahead at pit exit determine how much of that grip turns into time. A driver staying out can make an overcut work when the old tyre still has pace and the early stopper loses too much on the out-lap.

**Interaction.** Put two drivers on a lap-aligned timing strip. Choose **Stop first**, **Cover next lap**, or **Extend**, then set the old-tyre degradation, replacement compound, out-lap warm-up and pit-exit traffic. Advance past the second stop before scoring the position exchange. Show a gap ledger with separate terms for starting gap, tyre-pace advantage, warm-up, traffic and pit-loss difference. Offer a scrub to the first timing point both drivers cross after both stops. Then add an optional longer horizon for **Extend** so a later tyre-age advantage can be weighed against immediate track position. [Formula 1 undercut and overcut analysis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/jolyon-palmers-analysis-singapore-and-the-art-of-undercutting.1NgVyVsZnHTDEA9wi0s5lW)

**Worked challenge.** Driver B starts 0.8 s behind A. Both stops cost 20.0 s, counted once each and netting to zero. A’s comparison lap on old tyres lasts 90.0 s. B can run an equivalent 88.2 s on warm fresh tyres. Add 0.3 s warm-up and B gains 1.5 s and comes out 0.7 s ahead. A further 1.0 s in traffic leaves B 0.3 s behind. Remove the traffic and raise warm-up to 2.0 s and the fresh-tyre lap becomes 90.2 s, so B loses a further 0.2 s and stays 1.0 s behind. A warm-up + traffic cost of 1.0 s exactly leaves the cars level at the comparison point. Treat the simplified window as one clean equivalent-lap term, with stop terms accounted separately. In a measured replay, do not subtract a pit-time term twice from a lap that already includes it.

**Event evidence and telemetry.** Pirelli reported Ferrari and Gasly moved their first stops forward to use the undercut at Austria 2026. Its Las Vegas preview describes why a long straight after pit exit can cool tyres and weaken the undercut. These examples support paired hot-degradation and cool-out-lap fixtures. Use timed sectors, lap/position history, pits, nominated compounds and visible nearby cars. A warm-up trace based only on public timing is a model inference. Annotate neutralisations and obvious traffic, and avoid attributing the whole observed delta to tyre temperature. [Pirelli comments at Austria](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-the-teams-said-race-day-in-austria-2026.2c6WyTl6yjshJrSwqRsYAK) · [Las Vegas tyre preview](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-tyres-will-the-teams-and-drivers-have-for-the-2026-las-vegas-grand-prix.1HEaoIW48MVZs9cAT7rP2w) · [FIA Las Vegas race lap analysis](https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/2026_21_usa_f1_r0_timing_racelapanalysis_v01.pdf)

**Open question.** Can a highlighted pair of public laps support matched sector comparisons without an undocumented tyre temperature or track evolution assumption? If needed, reserve inference for the explanation and use the controlled fixture to validate causality.

## 14. Pack the fuel and tyres you will need at the finish

**Aha.** A strategy can be quick on paper and infeasible because it spends scarce tyres or finishes without a recoverable fuel sample. Starting fuel mass and fuel-saving decisions affect time, while tyre allocation tracks physical sets and race eligibility tracks specifications. These are separate ledgers.

**Interaction.** In the garage, assign physical tyre sets to practice, qualifying and the race. Show compound code, event-relative label, fresh/used state and whether the set is reserved or returned. Build a dry stint plan, select a start fuel load and allocate lift-and-coast laps. Replay mass, cumulative burn, reserve, projected loss, stint age and weather. Change the race to one where the learner actually uses an intermediate to lift the dry-compound constraint. A forecast toggle alone should leave eligibility unchanged. Offer a standard/Sprint allocation switch and enforce only the pinned edition’s return/reserve rules. [FIA Section B Issue 09, B5.1.4 and B6.1–3](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf)

**Rules and event evidence.** In the standard 2026 table, each driver is ordinarily allocated 2 Hard, 3 Medium and 8 Soft sets. Sprint format uses 2 Hard, 4 Medium and 6 Soft, with separate intermediate and wet availability and use caps. FIA/supplier exceptions and tyre tests can alter availability. A dry Grand Prix normally requires two different dry specifications including at least one nominated mandatory race specification when the driver has used no intermediate or wet. In 2026 Las Vegas the labels Hard/Medium/Soft mapped to C3/C4/C5. The FIA’s Pirelli competition note version applicable to each weekend should govern event prescriptions. Race fuel cannot be changed after leaving the pit lane for the specified start procedure under B5.1.4. C6.5.2 requires a recoverable 0.70-litre sample during the 2026 competition. The 3000 MJ/h fuel-energy-flow ceiling in C5.2.3 is an instantaneous rate. Use the 2021 Hungary finding of only 0.3 L recoverable under a then 1.0 L requirement as a dated case, without assuming the 2021 failure was simply a starting-load choice. [FIA 2026 Section B](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf) · [FIA 2026 Section C Issue 21](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [Las Vegas compound selection](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-tyres-will-the-teams-and-drivers-have-for-the-2026-las-vegas-grand-prix.1HEaoIW48MVZs9cAT7rP2w) · [FIA Las Vegas decision documents, including tyre notes V3](https://www.fia.com/documents/championships/fia-formula-one-world-championship-14/season/season-2026-2072/event/Las%20Vegas%20Grand%20Prix) · [2021 Hungary fuel decision](https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/doc_59_-_2021_hungarian_grand_prix_-_offence_-_car_5_-_fuel_.pdf)

**Worked challenge.** In a fictional 50-lap race, load 65.20 kg and budget 1.30 kg per lap, leaving 0.20 kg. Declare illustrative density 0.75 kg/L, which converts 0.70 L to 0.525 kg. Assume ideal recovery and zero inaccessible fuel only for the exercise. Four lift-and-coast laps saving 0.10 kg each leave 0.60 kg, passing the exercise with 0.075 kg spare. Three leave 0.50 kg and fail; the alternative is a heavier start. Charge 0.15 s per saving lap for this fixture, and give starting mass its own adjustable per-kg-per-lap cost so the tradeoff can move. Assign M1 then a nominated mandatory H1 to pass the dry strategy. Replacing H1 with a fresh M2 fails even though both sets were available. Mark the burn, density, recovery assumption and pace coefficient as teaching inputs.

**Open question.** Public timing and FIA inspection papers can establish stops, tyre prescriptions and documented sample checks. They do not reveal a reliable lap-by-lap fuel ledger or a team’s live nominated tyre inventory. Use synthetic mass/burn/temperature assumptions and show uncertainty in reserve and pace, with the measured event references in a separate expandable panel.
