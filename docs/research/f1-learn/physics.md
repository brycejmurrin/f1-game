# Race-car physics for interactive lessons

Use one clearly labeled illustrative single-seater preset and SI internally. Convert speed to km/h and acceleration to g for labels. Display requested and achieved values separately, with force and energy units on tooltips. Measured telemetry, a closed-form model, and synthetic demonstration data need distinct provenance in the interface.

## Tyre grip and combined slip

- **Interaction.** Provide signed slip ratio, signed slip angle, vertical load and surface grip sliders. Link two pure-slip curves to a force-plane plot with a combined demand vector and a projected vector. A second preset can demonstrate power on exit or trail braking.
- **Sign convention.** For forward rolling in wheel coordinates, take +x forward and +y left. Use longitudinal slip ratio k = (Rω − vx)/max(vx, ε), and slip angle α = atan2(−vy, max(vx, ε)). Positive k drives, negative k brakes. The chosen α sign gives positive force for positive α with positive cornering stiffness. At very low speed explain that slip estimates are ill-conditioned and disable the moving-wheel example or indicate the regularization.
- **Teaching model.** Per wheel and axis, Fpeak = μref Fref (Fz/Fref)^n with illustrative 0 < n ≤ 1. Use trial forces Fx0 = Fpeak,x tanh(Kx k/Fpeak,x) and Fy0 = Fpeak,y tanh(Ky α/Fpeak,y). Set forces to zero before division if Fz ≤ 0 or grip is zero. Let u = hypot(Fx0/Fpeak,x, Fy0/Fpeak,y) and project each trial force by dividing it by max(1,u). This steady-state envelope has no post-peak drop, transient relaxation, camber, thermal, or surface evolution. The force ellipse depicts capacity. Actual combined-slip curves depend on test data and tyre model coefficients. Add a separate explicitly illustrative post-peak curve if teaching locked-wheel degradation, or eventually use fitted combined-slip data. [Brach & Brach, SAE force ellipse analysis](https://doi.org/10.4271/2011-01-0094), [Pacejka & Bakker, Magic Formula](https://doi.org/10.1080/00423119208969994), [Project Chrono tyre model documentation](https://api.projectchrono.org/wheeled_tire.html)
- **Checks.** With unit force limits and pure demands of 0.8 and 0.8, the projected coordinates are approximately 0.7071 on both axes. Zero slip gives zero model force. A 20% reduction in peak lateral capacity should reduce available lateral force at the same normalized demand by 20%. At Fref = 1000 N, μref = 1.5 and n = 0.9, two evenly loaded 1000 N tyres total 3000 N of peak capacity. Loads of 500 N and 1500 N total about 2964 N. With n = 1 both load distributions give 3000 N.

## Load transfer and braking

- **Interaction.** Use top and side views with four proportional wheel loads and force arrows. Sliders set longitudinal acceleration, lateral acceleration, centre-of-gravity height, and optionally front transfer share. Show brake bias overlaid against available front and rear braking. Reversing corner direction should swap left and right loads.
- **Model.** On flat ground, take positive ax forward. For wheelbase L = lf + lr, CG height h, front aerodynamic fraction b and positive downforce A, use Fzf = mg lr/L − m ax h/L + bA and Fzr = mg lf/L + m ax h/L + (1−b)A. This quasi-static pitch approximation neglects aerodynamic drag moments and assumes the longitudinal force producing the transfer is approximately m ax. For a no-aero equal-track model, the amount shifted from inside to outside is Δ = m |ay| h/t. Front and rear shares λΔ and (1−λ)Δ add to the outside wheel and subtract from the inside wheel of their respective axles. With unequal tracks, allocate roll moment λ m|ay|h and (1−λ)m|ay|h and divide each by its track width. Treat λ as an input proxy for suspension and geometry effects. If a wheel would lift, flag the model limit. [MIT Formula SAE dynamics thesis](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/139209/gaither-gaither-sb-2-2021-thesis.pdf), [Aston Formula Student dynamics thesis](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/race-car-vehicle-dynamics-and-design-applied-to-formula-student/)
- **Bias and lock threshold.** For requested positive total braking force B, front and rear request bbrake B and (1−bbrake)B. Compare to each axle’s available longitudinal force, reduced when cornering. If each tyre’s existing lateral demand is known, its remaining straight-axis capacity is Fx,peak sqrt(max(0, 1−(Fy/Fy,peak)^2)). The first axle to exceed its capacity indicates threshold order in this model. Call this an excess-demand or lock-risk indicator. To show actual ω reaching zero, integrate wheel dynamics I dω/dt = Tdrive − Tbrake − Fx R with tyre slip and stop logic. To illustrate braking points, use d = (v0²−v1²)/(2a) for constant positive deceleration magnitude, or integrate speed-dependent deceleration. On a modern hybrid F1 lesson, show rear braking as friction plus regeneration with a shared total rear demand. [Brembo Formula 1 rear brake-by-wire](https://www.brembo.com/en/motorsport/formula1), [F1 lockup terminology](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/a-beginners-guide-to-f1-slang.1Pg6tvGZ2y7u4KAnc8WXGl)
- **Checks.** For m = 800 kg, h = 0.30 m, L = 3.5 m, ax = −9.81 m/s², forward load shift is approximately 673 N. For ay = 9.81 m/s² and t = 1.6 m, total outside load gain is approximately 1472 N. All four wheel loads sum to mg + A. Braking from 200 to 100 km/h at constant 10 m/s² takes about 115.74 m.

## Aerodynamics and lap speed

- **Interaction.** Plot drag and downforce against speed, with a marker updated by speed, wing preset, or clean/wake-air scenario. A front-balance indicator should expose the front downforce share. In a linked corner example show required lateral acceleration v² |κ| and available grip. A higher-downforce setup can also have higher drag, depending on the prescribed coefficients.
- **Model.** In windless air with fixed coefficients, q = ½ρv², drag = q(CD S), and downforce A = q(|CL| S). Drag acts rearward in forward motion. Specify whether a slider changes coefficient or coefficient-area product. A simple wake scenario can scale the front and rear downforce independently within illustrative ranges. Wake changes, ride-height sensitivity, stall and yaw effects need maps or measured evidence to quantify. Avoid using a generic wake percentage as a claimed measurement. [NASA drag equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/drag-equation/), [NASA lift equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/lift-equation/), [Formula 1 dirty-air explanation](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/a-beginners-guide-to-f1-slang.1Pg6tvGZ2y7u4KAnc8WXGl)
- **Lap extension.** Sample the supplied line by distance, derive signed curvature and solve for maximum feasible local speed using the lateral grip budget. Run forward acceleration and backward braking passes using vNext² = v² + 2ax Δs, iterating across the seam for closed laps. Keep drag as a longitudinal force outside the tyre-force budget and constrain drive by available power/traction. Segment time under constant acceleration is 2Δs/(v + vNext) when the denominator is positive. Show where the limit is cornering, braking, traction, power or gearing. Cap at a declared speed ceiling. The simplified constant-μ aero model can otherwise lack a finite cornering root when lift-induced grip grows as fast as the lateral demand. [TUM quasi-steady lap simulator](https://github.com/TUMFTM/laptime-simulation), [TUM trajectory optimisation](https://github.com/TUMFTM/global_racetrajectory_optimization)
- **Checks.** At the same density and coefficients, double speed gives four times the aero forces and eight times the power required against drag. At zero curvature lateral demand is zero. With zero aero and constant μ, circular limit equals sqrt(μg/|κ|). For μ = 1.5 and radius 50 m, this gives ~27.12 m/s. Time and speeds should converge as track sample spacing shrinks.

## Drivetrain and energy

- **Interaction.** Link engine torque versus RPM, tractive force versus road speed by gear, shift crossings, driven-axle grip and drag. Expose the conditions that determine whether a lower gear helps.
- **Model.** For gear ratio G, final drive D, tyre radius R, efficiency η, and no clutch or tyre slip, use ωe = vGD/R, Te = torqueCurve(ωe), Fwheel = TeGDη/R. Road acceleration is (min(Fwheel, driven-axle available Fx) − Faero − Froll)/m. Engine RPM = 60 ωe/(2π). Use only gears within RPM range and account for shift duration if comparing acceleration times. At zero speed, use a launch/clutch approximation or begin the example at rolling speed. If hybrid is included, define electrical power conventions explicitly. For terminal stored-energy model Enew = clamp(E + (ηrec Prec − Pdep/ηdep)Δt, 0, Emax), interpret Prec and Pdep at the mechanical side, and limit applied power by remaining energy or charge space before integration. [OpenLAP vehicle model](https://github.com/mc12027/OpenLAP-Lap-Time-Simulator/blob/master/OpenVEHICLE.m), [TUM hybrid lap simulation](https://github.com/TUMFTM/laptime-simulation)
- **Checks.** With 200 N·m, total ratio 8, efficiency 0.9, radius 0.3 m, wheel force is 4800 N. At 30 m/s engine speed is 800 rad/s or ~7639 rpm and wheel power is 144 kW, matching η Te ωe. The zero-acceleration road-speed intersection occurs when applied wheel force equals resistance. Empty battery prevents deployment and a full battery limits storage from regeneration.
- **Season context.** Mark aero mode, hybrid rules, and sporting tyre requirements with the season and source revision. Keep generic educational presets separate from regulated configurations. The FIA index listed 2026 Section C Issue 21 dated 19 October 2026 when checked. [FIA Formula One regulations index](https://www.fia.com/regulations/category/110)

## Pit strategy

- **Interaction.** Compare two stint timelines with compound, tyre age, pit laps, cumulative gap, and lead changes. Let users vary overtaking traffic, warm-up, pit loss, and neutralisation. Label the time origin, whether a stop penalty lands on in-lap or out-lap, and whether gap is positive when the selected driver is behind.
- **Model.** Use per-lap time = baseline + fuel mass penalty + compound/age penalty + warm-up penalty + traffic penalty + pit lane time loss. A transparent starting stint approximation is deg × age plus an optional post-threshold nonlinear penalty. Use tyre age zero for the first lap on a set and advance once after each lap. Set pit loss relative to comparable laps without the stop and add it once. A Safety Car or VSC can reduce the relative loss, with session timing and traffic modeled explicitly if teaching real races. An undercut’s recovered time includes the fresh-tyre out-lap versus old-tyre laps, warm-up, traffic, and any difference in stops. Vary pit laps and available compounds across eligible strategies, and add scenarios or seeded Monte Carlo for uncertainty. [TUM race simulation](https://github.com/TUMFTM/race-simulation), [Heilmeier et al., stochastic race simulation](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10124229), [Formula 1 undercut and overcut example](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/jolyon-palmers-analysis-singapore-and-the-art-of-undercutting.1NgVyVsZnHTDEA9wi0s5lW)
- **Checks.** With equal pit-loss terms, a one-lap undercut where the old tyre runs 90.0 s and the replacement runs 88.5 s gains 1.5 s over the comparison interval once both drivers have pitted. Adding a 2.0 s traffic penalty makes it lose 0.5 s. Strategies with identical lap terms must give identical finish time. Disabling all penalties leaves only baseline lap-time accumulation. In a stochastic comparison use shared random scenarios across plans and report quantiles plus scenario count.
