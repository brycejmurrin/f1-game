## 4. Energy deployment, Overtake and clipping

**Aha.** A driver can ask for power and have charge left while the permitted electrical power falls. A different driver can have the same permission and speed but run out of energy. The two traces may both show falling acceleration and require different fixes.

**Explore.** Reuse Early/Late/Both and the 0.85/1.10 s gap switch from the existing exercise. Add one optional replay of the late window that overlays requested electrical DC kW, legally permitted DC kW, delivered DC kW and usable charge in MJ. Keep a cursor at the instant power falls. Show the limiter that bound delivery at that point. An inactive dry race lap has a detection event and a separate activation event. Changes to the current gap between those events do not change the stored observation. With Overtake active, the instantaneous electrical allowance rises at high speed. Up to 0.5 MJ additional *recharge allowance* may also be available under the relevant race conditions. It is neither a battery refill nor a blanket additional deployment allowance. [FIA 2026 Technical C5.2.7–10](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [FIA 2026 Sporting B7.2](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf)

**Vocabulary.** In teaching copy, call the reduction in permitted positive electrical delivery a **speed-dependent limit** and call running out of stored energy **charge depleted**. Fans and drivers may use *clipping* loosely for the loss of electric assistance on a straight. Keep those causes visually distinct and reserve **full-throttle harvesting** or **superclipping** for negative MGU-K operation with the accelerator still fully pressed in module 5. **Overtake** is the rule-dependent use of a different delivery curve and conditional extra recharge. **Boost** is the separate driver override of power-demand strategy, subject to restrictions. **Straight Mode** reduces drag through active aerodynamics under a separate rule and has its own circuit zones. [FIA C5.12 and B7](https://www.fia.com/regulation/category/2182) · [FIA Miami refinements](https://www.fia.com/news/refinements-2026-fia-formula-1-regulations-agreed-all-stakeholders)

**Live view.** Plot time or lap distance, speed, pedal input, game battery state and Boost request where genuinely exported, and markers for activation and energy windows if the scenario actually provides them. Use a separate modeled rules overlay driven by speed and a declared regulatory fixture. Label power and binding reason as modeled whenever they are derived rather than reported by the runtime. The game currently documents speed, longitudinal acceleration, battery behavior and game Boost, plus separate aerodynamic-zone behavior. Verify each new field at the actual scenario entry point before tagging it **Apex 26 run**. [Apex 26 physics](../../PHYSICS.md) · [Apex 26 observations](../../DEBUG-HOOKS.md)

**Challenge and acceptance.** Ask the learner to diagnose two late-straight plateaus and choose the appropriate response. At 340 km/h for 3 s with 1.10 MJ and no Overtake, standard delivery is limited to 100 kW and 0.30 MJ. Saving more cannot increase delivery. At the same speed with Overtake and 0.40 MJ remaining, the 300 kW allowance would support 0.90 MJ, but actual delivery is 0.40 MJ and ends at 1.333… s. Saving early can fix the latter. Boundary-check standard/Overtake full-power edges at 290/337.5 km/h and zero edges at 345/355 km/h. At exactly 1.00 s the fresh, inactive Las Vegas-style fixture fails the strict less-than initial activation test. B7 has additional carry-over and safety rules. Include a source note that the dry base curves and illustrative fixture do not apply to all sectors, circuits or sessions. In Low Grip, Overtake is disabled and speed curves can change. [Las Vegas PU bulletin, 17 November](https://www.fia.com/system/files/decision-document/2026_las_vegas_grand_prix_-_power_unit_information.pdf)

## 5. Lap battery budget and recovery choices

**Aha.** A fast qualifying lap can spend charge built up beforehand. In a repeatable race map, each complete lap needs to recover approximately as much energy as it spends unless the driver intends to draw down or build reserve over multiple laps.

**Explore.** Give each of three sector cards a deployment setting and a **Lift earlier** toggle. Deployment sets the desired total draw in the following acceleration window and earlier lift replaces a portion of powered running with a larger recovery opportunity in the following braking approach. Advance within each sector from deployment to recovery. Plot both events on the same usable state-of-charge curve and keep the horizontal start-charge line visible. Show energy spent, actually recovered, finish charge and the charge-neutrality result. The first target is a repeatable race lap that delivers at least 1.25 MJ and finishes within 0.01 MJ of its start charge. The second target is an illustrative qualifying burn that delivers 1.80 MJ with a finish reserve of at least 0.30 MJ. Opening charge is 1.40 MJ for both, with a 4.00 MJ usable window. The qualifying preset is a one-lap allocation of that opening reserve.

**Worked ledger.** All values are MJ at the same idealized battery reference and rounded here for display. Use these values as fixture targets for the setup above. Each row spends first, then recovers. Additional lift opportunities are illustrative and do not establish a real time penalty.

Use achieved draw and recovery in the ledger. For each sample, actual draw is limited by available charge and actual recovery by remaining headroom. An additional per-lap recharge restriction can be covered in the explainer. At a capped step, show requested and actual values separately and use **Charge depleted** or **Store full** in the corresponding state. The example models DC energy on one idealized bus. The FIA measures recharge over a lap at the CU-K HV DC bus and separately sets a 4 MJ usable state-of-charge swing. Las Vegas illustrates session differences with 6.0 MJ qualifying recharge and 8.0/8.5 MJ in the race without/with Overtake. [FIA C5.2.7–10](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [FIA Las Vegas PU bulletin](https://www.fia.com/system/files/decision-document/2026_las_vegas_grand_prix_-_power_unit_information.pdf)

**Superclipping context.** A third way to recover energy can occur with accelerator demand held at 100%. Negative MGU-K torque loads the powertrain and diverts some ICE output into the store. While recovery reduces available propulsion, the car can still accelerate if propulsion exceeds road load. Illustrate with 400 kW ICE and 200 kW generator load for 2 s at an idealized common reference plane. That leaves 200 kW for propulsion and adds 0.40 MJ before losses and capacity limits. Keep this as an explainer or replay callout so the primary controls remain deployment and lift. The FIA's April package raised peak superclip power from 250 to 350 kW under its announced conditions to reduce charging time and adjusted qualifying recharge. [FIA Miami package](https://www.fia.com/news/refinements-2026-fia-formula-1-regulations-agreed-all-stakeholders)

**Live view and challenge.** Overlay actual throttle, brake, speed, game battery state and game sector plan if exported at the scenario entry point. Derive channel deltas as labeled game observations only where the source defines their units and meaning. A rise in plotted charge near full accelerator does not independently establish FIA superclipping. First ask for a repeatable lap. Reducing sector 3 recovery from 0.80 to 0.30 in the worked race map gives a 0.90 MJ finish and a 0.50 MJ deficit despite identical requested deployment. Then let the learner switch to qualifying and see why the 0.30 MJ finish requires an independently replenished starting reserve to repeat. Verify every intermediate point and total, the 0.00 MJ qualifying trough, no negative charge, and a recovery request of 0.30 MJ at 3.85 MJ storing only 0.15 MJ. ## 6. Rear brake blending and corner-exit torque fill

**Aha.** Under braking, changing regeneration can change the caliper's share without changing rear braking demand. Under acceleration, adding electric torque can make the car respond sooner while the turbo continues to spool on the same curve.

### Rear brake blending

**Explore.** Hold pedal and speed fixed and show a positive **1,000 Nm rear wheel-equivalent braking target**, composed of engine braking, MGU-K regeneration and rear caliper torque. Keep the engine-braking contribution fixed at 150 Nm for the first pass. Give the learner a regen control from 0–600 Nm plus a **Battery full** switch. Use caliper command equal to the remaining rear target after subtracting achieved engine braking and regen. At 600 Nm regen the caliper supplies 250 Nm. At 200 Nm regen it supplies 650 Nm. A full battery sets accepted regen to zero in this fixture and increases caliper torque to 850 Nm, leaving engine braking at 150 Nm and total rear braking at 1,000 Nm. A second example at full battery changes engine braking to 300 Nm and yields 700 Nm caliper torque. Keep the independent front request fixed at 2,000 Nm. Torque figures refer to the same wheel-equivalent plane. Use demanded friction and a visible unmet-target state if a future version has a hydraulic or grip cap, rather than silently exceeding it.

**Live view and challenge.** Ask which channel should replace the disappearing regeneration with the same pedal input. Accept an increase from 250 to 850 Nm in the first fixture, with constant engine braking and constant total. In a live comparison plot brake, throttle, speed, longitudinal acceleration and charge only where exported. The game models brake stability using a re-split and does not establish measured rear caliper, engine-braking or generator torques through that mechanism. Use the model for the three-way decomposition unless the runtime independently supplies it. A real F1 rear brake controller may use powered rear pressure under C11.6, which retains hydraulic fallback and limits powered rear pressure to 1.2 times contemporaneous rear master cylinder pressure. C11.4 prohibits systems designed to prevent wheel lock. The 500 Nm MGU-K limit is crankshaft-referenced and cannot be compared directly with the illustrative wheel-equivalent values. [FIA C5.2.11, C11.1, C11.4 and C11.6](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [Mercedes-AMG F1 brake systems](https://www.mercedesamgf1.com/news/formula-one-brake-systems-explained) · [Apex 26 physics and telemetry](../../PHYSICS.md)

### Corner-exit torque fill

**Explore.** Use an illustrative rolling corner exit with fixed gear and engine speed for the short comparison. Draw the pedal step, normalized spool/boost, crank-equivalent ICE contribution, MGU-K fill and combined torque. Set sampled ICE crank-equivalent torque to 120, 240 and 360 Nm at 0, 0.5 and 1.0 s after the step. Use an illustrative **300 Nm requested total**, a **120 Nm fill cap**, and linear interpolation between samples. With fill off, delivered samples can be displayed capped to the request (120, 240, 300 Nm), and with fill on they are 240, 300, 300 Nm. Request full torque is reached at 0.75 s without fill and 0.25 s with it. The separate uncapped ICE capability and spool trace are identical in both. The fixture omits changes to ICE torque demand, boost dynamics and grip, and time-to-torque is a modeled metric. [Formula 1 turbo lag and 2026 race starts](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/explained-why-race-starts-will-be-different-in-2026.7ADR4zkfl299T7i3ZYVTYK) · [Official hybrid PU explainer](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/2026-regulations-explained-all-you-necessitate-to-know-about-f1s-new-power-units.14jfv7a36905uDJDdNyfQd)

**Architecture and motion.** The 2014–25 MGU-H shared the turbocharger system and could use electricity to spin the shaft or recover energy from it. In the 2026 architecture the exhaust turbine drives the compressor without an MGU-H. The crank-linked MGU-K can assist the driveline when allowed by speed, mechanical torque, DC power, charge and controls. The example isolates torque fill to teach the concept. Spool response in a real integrated PU can change with gear, engine speed, exhaust mass flow, throttle and calibration. A separate **Standing start** card shows why prior spool matters. C5.2.12 generally permits MGU-K use only once the car reaches 50 km/h on a standing start, with Standard ECU minimum-acceleration and post-start-signal stall-prevention exceptions. These automatic safety interventions differ from freely deployable torque fill. [FIA 2026 Technical C5.2, C5.3, C5.16 and C5.18](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [FIA October approval](https://www.fia.com/news/rally-roadmaps-and-grassroots-affordability-focus-fia-world-motor-sport-council)

**Live view.** Use pedal, speed, gear or engine RPM where the driving scenario provides them and align these with longitudinal acceleration and battery state. Treat ACCEL response as an outcome affected by gear, grip, drag and grade. Confirm whether there is a physical spool/boost state and a separate ICE/MGU-K torque split before attributing any run to turbo lag. Engine audio exposes a turbo-style whine and hybrid layers. These are sound channels and cannot establish compressor pressure or shaft RPM. If either raw torque split or spool is absent, keep the synchronized decomposition in the illustrative model. [Apex 26 debug and audio hooks](../../DEBUG-HOOKS.md)

**Challenge and acceptance.** Choose fill on a rolling exit with enough charge and explain what changed. Reset at zero charge and confirm the no-fill curve, then show a normal standing-start sample below 50 km/h with no automatic intervention active and zero manual K assistance. Above the threshold, show eligibility as conditional, rather than depicting guaranteed positive torque. Preserve one unit/shaft reference across summed torque. A later physics version could convert torque to power with shaft angular speed and gearbox ratios, apply efficiency and charge/torque constraints, and validate against instrumented data. The present exercise uses no manufacturer turbo map, team calibration or predicted launch performance.

## Open validation for implementation

Before building any live challenge, inspect the current game's scenario injection and readback surfaces for brake and throttle input timing, detection/activation state, regeneration, stored energy units, and whether axle/PU decomposition exists. Record which traces can be exported and identify each as game telemetry, an FIA-rule overlay or an illustrative fixture. The texts above specify complete standalone lesson tests. Authentic wet operation, power-demand ramps across zones and real turbo or hydraulic timing require additional published event data or explicit game instrumentation.
