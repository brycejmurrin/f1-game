# Candidate Learn modules 7–11 · Driving dynamics

These briefs describe curriculum options. The first playable example in each uses labeled illustrative data. The existing Grip & Braking lesson covers a fixed-load force envelope. Module 8 extends that model, and module 10 can build on an existing trace fixture. ## 7. Thermal windows, graining and blistering

**Aha.** A tyre can warm quickly at the surface while its bulk remains cold. Heat history and sliding affect what the driver feels. Pirelli describes graining as a surface phenomenon influenced by operating conditions and sliding, and blistering as damage associated with local overheating. Cold sliding and prolonged heating are useful examples. Their effects depend on the tyre and conditions. [Pirelli motorsport chief engineer on graining](https://www.pirelli.com/global/en-ww/race/racingspot/formula-1/it-s-graining-men--124082/), [Pirelli on local heating and blistering](https://press.pirelli.com/the-grand-prix-of-europe-from-a-tyre-point-of-view/), [Farroni et al., Thermo Racing Tyre model](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11012-013-9821-9)

**Experiment.** Scrub through a synthetic out-lap at 70°C on both channels. Compare a smooth load history with a slide at the same start state, then enter a cooling segment. Plot surface and bulk temperature in °C, time in seconds, an illustrative operating band and markers for sliding or sustained heat. Give grip and defect indicators independent readouts. In the simple lag fixture, both nodes approach 100°C with time constants of 9 and 35 s. After 9 s the surface is 89.0°C and the bulk is 76.8°C. This fixture illustrates lag. Physical models add heat generation, heat exchange and coupling between layers. [Thermal lab states](driving-sessions.md#lab-2-follow-the-water-and-temperature), [Temperature-dependent tyre friction in a race-car model](https://doi.org/10.1080/00423114.2011.622406)

**Engineer Challenge.** Predict which temperature will leave the example operating band first during a short cooling segment. Reveal why a cool surface can coexist with residual bulk heat. Then show two runs with the same current surface reading and ask which experienced a cold slide. Accept “insufficient evidence” until the event history appears.

**Open research.** Verify temperature-channel provenance and which values the game estimates. Decide how to represent defect growth and recovery. Real compound windows and blanket limits require dated, product-specific evidence. Current temperature alone leaves damage diagnosis uncertain.

## 8. Braking, load transfer and the friction circle

**Aha.** Higher braking demand leaves less lateral force available. Deceleration increases front vertical load and decreases rear load. Brake bias sets the requested split. Excess demand depends on each axle's load, tyre behavior and lateral demand. [Brach & Brach, applicability of the force ellipse](https://doi.org/10.4271/2011-01-0094), [MIT Formula SAE load-transfer analysis](https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/139209), [Brembo on Formula 1 rear braking](https://www.brembo.com/en/motorsport/formula1)

**Experiment.** Link a side view to front and rear force plots. Freeze a specified deceleration and vary bias to identify the smaller axle margin. Display load in N, tyre force in kN, deceleration in g and front bias in percent. With no aero and zero lateral demand, an illustrative 800 kg car with 47% static front share, 0.30 m CG height and 3.5 m wheelbase transfers about 673 N forward at 1 g. The loads become 4361 N front and 3487 N rear. At μ=1, equal bias requests 3924 N per axle and exceeds rear capacity by 437 N. A later speed experiment can add downforce under fixed coefficients and a prescribed front aero share. [Worked braking case](driving-sessions.md#lab-1-spend-grip-through-a-braking-zone), [NASA lift equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/lift-equation/)

**Engineer Challenge.** Find the bias that reaches both axle capacities at the prescribed 1 g. Accept approximately 55.6% front. Add front lateral demand and ask which margin falls first. Call the result excess demand or lock risk. Actual wheel lock requires zero wheel rotation while the car is still moving.

**Open research.** Choose where drag acts and whether to model its pitch moment. A dynamic version needs to solve achieved deceleration and transfer together. Calibrate tyre load sensitivity and aero balance before making real-car predictions. Game lock cues and rear hydraulic/regenerative blending have separate models. [Physics notes](physics.md#load-transfer-and-braking), [Brembo on 2026 brake blending](https://www.brembo.com/en/motorsport/formula1/f1-rules-2026)

## 9. Racing lines and apex geometry

**Aha.** The kerb, closest point to the inside, maximum curvature and minimum speed can occur at different distances. The line trades distance, curvature, exit speed and positioning for the next section. Minimum curvature is only an approximation to minimum time. [TUM global race-trajectory optimisation](https://github.com/TUMFTM/global_racetrajectory_optimization), [TUM quasi-steady lap simulator](https://github.com/TUMFTM/laptime-simulation)

**Experiment.** Draw a bounded 90-degree turn followed by either a short link or a straight. Let players adjust turn-in or choose early, middle and late apex presets. Mark closest approach separately from peak curvature and minimum speed. Show curvature in 1/m, distance in m, speed in km/h and elapsed time to the same gates. Fix entry speed, grip and boundaries at first. With no aero, μ=1.2 and a 50 m radius, the steady lateral limit is approximately 87.3 km/h. A 40 m radius gives 78.1 km/h. Add a forward/backward speed pass for a whole-corner time comparison. [Lap-speed assumptions](physics.md#aerodynamics-and-lap-speed)

**Engineer Challenge.** Lengthen only the exit straight. In a transparent counterexample, one line loses 0.20 s by the exit gate but leaves at 44 m/s versus 40 m/s. Hold the respective exit speeds for 200 m and it gains about 0.45 s on the straight, winning overall by 0.25 s. With 50 m of straight it still loses about 0.09 s. Ask which line wins at each gate and reveal the cumulative delta. Holding exit speeds is a teaching assumption, and a fuller model would let both cars accelerate.

**Open research.** Choose curvature-continuous paths and valid track-boundary constraints. Verify both cars clear the same exit gate and that their full footprints stay within the fixture limits. The fast line depends on the corner sequence, the car and the interval being timed.

## 10. Telemetry Detective

**Aha.** A later brake onset can gain time at turn-in while a lower minimum speed loses more by the exit. A distance-aligned delta locates the loss or gain. Multiple driving or track conditions can produce the same pattern. [MoTeC i2 graph and time variance views](https://www.motec.com.au/products/I2), [OpenF1 channel definitions](https://openf1.org/docs/#car-data)

**Experiment.** Start paused on two synthetic traces. Toggle time and estimated distance with a shared cursor and markers for brake on, minimum observed speed and throttle recovery. Provide a table of values and an explicit sign convention, B minus A. The 10–90 m fixture gives B a later observed brake event, a 126 rather than 144 km/h minimum observed speed, and a +0.133 s segment delta. Reverse the reference to flip the sign. Indicate which markers come from raw samples and which use interpolation. OpenF1 brake is pressed or released. Those samples do not establish pressure, the friction/regen split, or the geometric apex. [Reproducible fixture](telemetry-ideas.md#worked-fixture), [OpenF1 car data](https://openf1.org/docs/#car-data)

**Engineer Challenge.** Predict who reaches 90 m first with only the brake strip visible, then reveal the speed and time delta. Accept conclusions tied to observed patterns and an “insufficient evidence” answer for exact cause. Demonstrate an unavailable result if an alignment gap or an uncovered endpoint compromises the comparison.

**Open research.** Verify missing-data handling before the selected window and confirm provider reuse terms and metadata before bundling real traces. Keep the synthetic case available offline. An optional Data Hub bridge needs verified return focus and session state. Official event timing supplies its own lap and sector reference. [FIA Las Vegas event timing](https://www.fia.com/events/fia-formula-one-world-championship/season-2026/las-vegas-grand-prix/eventtiming-information), [Telemetry interpretation and integration](telemetry-ideas.md#interpretation-for-real-sessions)

## 11. Rain lines and aquaplaning

**Aha.** Reduced wet friction, aquaplaning in standing water and limited visibility are distinct mechanisms. Local surface and water conditions change the best line, and an alternative path still has to fit the corner geometry. There is no universal wet line or aquaplaning speed. [Formula 1 wet driving example](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/watch-the-challenge-of-wet-weather-driving-and-how-verstappen-and-norris.1D3ZJJlakbWe2nlaFp8cXI), [NASA hydroplaning experiments](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19640000612), [NASA on depth, texture and tread](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19650033238)

**Experiment.** Use a two-route corner map with a local water overlay. Toggle dry, damp and standing-water segments independently of rain intensity. Display the assumed local μ, radius in m, estimated lateral limit in km/h and a separate standing-water multiplier. At equal 50 m radius, the illustrative damp racing line at μ=0.75 gives 69.0 km/h. An off-line strip at μ=0.90 gives 75.6 km/h. Tighten the off-line path to 40 m and its limit becomes 67.7 km/h, reversing the ranking. In a separate standing-water preset, keep base wet friction fixed while varying tread and speed. Label Apex thresholds as game-specific. [Worked line and aquaplaning cases](driving-sessions.md#lab-2-follow-the-water-and-temperature), [Pirelli on wet tyre crossover](https://www.pirelli.com/global/en-ww/race/racingspot/formula-1/when-it-s-time-to-change-from-slick-to-wet-tyres-in-formula-1-52943/)

**Engineer Challenge.** Give a drying lap with an isolated pond on the nominal line. Ask the learner to pick a route and speed together. Accept multiple pairs inside the stated model limits, and reveal how the pond, path radius and available grip affect each. Keep spray visibility in its own readout.

**Open research.** Choose between authored local water and a calibrated track model. Verify game support for local conditions and characterize tread, pressure and depth before quoting real-world onsets. Runway experiments explain the mechanism under their own test conditions.
