## Lead case · Did leaving the track preserve an advantage?

**Learning outcome.** Identify what entitled the attacking driver to space in the stewards’ published analysis, then distinguish the defender’s decision to avoid contact from the sporting consequence of gaining and retaining a position advantage. Plan for a two-minute first pass and an optional transfer question.

**Historical record.** During the race on 21 November 2026, Lando Norris (car 1) attempted a pass on the left of Lewis Hamilton (car 44) approaching Turn 7. The stewards found Norris’s front axle at least alongside Hamilton’s mirror at the apex and determined Norris was entitled to the corner under the Driving Standards Guidelines. Hamilton remained alongside, crossed the right-hand white line at Turn 8, avoided a potential collision and stayed ahead. The stewards found a lasting advantage because Hamilton did not concede the place. Document 54 records a 10-second penalty for a breach of B1.8.6. It records video, timing and in-car video as the reviewed evidence. Keep the chapter’s text paraphrased, use the original decision as the reveal source, and depict relative order and qualitative alignment without claiming to reproduce actual GPS, telemetry or camera views. [FIA Las Vegas Doc 54](https://www.fia.com/system/files/decision-document/2026_las_vegas_grand_prix_-_infringement_-_car_44_-_leaving_the_track_and_gaining_an_advantage.pdf)

### Learn interaction

1. **Inspect the sequence.** Start at the approach. Buttons labelled **Approach**, **Apex**, **White line**, **Rejoin**, **Position retained** reveal authored frames with a shared textual readout. Highlight the two relevant features at the apex with labels **Car 1 front axle** and **Car 44 mirror**. At Turn 8 distinguish the line on the drawing from the asserted fact that car 44 left the track. When marking the line crossing, describe the cars by name or number rather than colour alone.
2. **Decide what the evidence supports.** Offer “The lasting advantage finding is supported,” “Avoiding contact resolves the advantage question,” and “Crossing the line is sufficient to show a lasting advantage.” Ask the learner to choose any two event findings as evidence. Credit **left the track** and **stayed ahead of the entitled attacker after rejoining**. An optional third card, **Norris was entitled to the corner**, gives context. Keep “the speed trace shows a gain of 0.2 s” out of the interaction because no such trace is supplied.
3. **Reveal the reasoning.** Start with the proposition the learner chose. Then explain that avoiding contact accounted for why Hamilton went wide. The stewards examined what he retained afterward. Reinforce that B1.8.6 governs safe rejoining and lasting advantage, and that the guidelines support judgment about entitlement. Explain that the inside-pass guidance considers the front axle alongside the mirror **before and at the apex**, control of the car and a line within track limits. The selected frame represents the published apex observation, while the entitlement finding comes from the stewards’ full review. Place the specific sanction behind **Show official decision** so the score concerns reasoning. [FIA Sporting B1.8.6, Issue 09](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf) · [FIA Driving Standards Guidelines](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/2026_f1_driving_standards_guidelines.pdf) · [FIA Las Vegas Doc 54](https://www.fia.com/system/files/decision-document/2026_las_vegas_grand_prix_-_infringement_-_car_44_-_leaving_the_track_and_gaining_an_advantage.pdf)
4. **Read the record.** Reveal **10-second time penalty** with the source and distinct labels for **incident order** and **final classification**. A collapsed card can show Norris P2 and Hamilton P7 in the final classification. The classification is a point-in-time official result. Skip simulated position swaps or a claim that ten seconds was the only change between the incident and the final order. [FIA event final classification](https://www.fia.com/system/files/decision-document/2026_las_vegas_grand_prix_-_final_race_classification.pdf)
5. **Try a transfer scenario.** Present a fictional alternate case with every other input fixed. Car 44 takes the escape route and then gives back the position when safe, at the Race Director’s discretion, with the fixture expressly specifying that no other advantage continues. Expected answer **The evidence for a retained position advantage has been removed.** Follow with a distinction between that limited conclusion and a decision about any other incident. B1.8.6 gives the Race Director discretion to offer an opportunity to return the whole advantage. The screen should not present an automatic amnesty or rewrite the historical decision. [FIA Sporting B1.8.6, Issue 09](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf)

### Accessible states and acceptance fixtures

Bundle the sequence and short evidence descriptions so the case still runs offline. Put the full text sequence alongside or beneath the diagram, with **Previous** and **Next** buttons and a step indicator. Buttons select frames with one semantic current state. Use a fieldset for each choice group and a discrete review action. Move focus to the result heading after review, keep retry on the same visible landmark and avoid live announcements on every scrub event. Opening the source is optional, and skipping the question can reveal the official decision without recording a prediction score. Provide a static step mode as the default under reduced motion. Phone landscape and portrait layouts keep the active question and reveal control reachable without depending on a precise touch gesture. [Learn delivery and keyboard conventions](hub.md)

| Fixture | Inputs | Expected output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical evidence | Past approach, apex, boundary and rejoin; choose incident finding and two supported evidence cards | Correct feedback identifies the retained position and safe avoidance as separate observations; official panel reveals 10 seconds and Doc 54. |
| Incomplete explanation | Choose boundary crossing as sufficient proof, then submit | Feedback asks for evidence after rejoin. Selections survive review; retry can change the choice. |
| Evidence unavailable in a teaching fixture | Remove exit-order card from fictional fixture | Show **Insufficient evidence to determine whether the advantage continued**. Avoid a green “legal” result. Keep this separate from the loaded historical evidence. |
| Transfer assumption | Position returned safely and no other advantage persists by fixture declaration | Correct feedback says the retained position evidence is gone and leaves other findings open. Historical result remains 10 seconds. |
| Skip and reset | Reveal before answering, then start over | Historical result is reachable immediately through skip; no assessment score is created; reset clears answers, frames and expansion state. |
| Text and keyboard | Keyboard-only navigation at 200% UI scale, narrow landscape and portrait, reduced motion enabled | Reach all evidence and both reveal stages; frame labels and current position agree with the drawing; focus enters result on submit; no animation or source link is required to answer. |

## Expansion after the first case

Use Las Vegas Doc 61 as the next comparison. Sergio Pérez’s front axle did not reach Rafael Câmara’s mirror during the move, the cars collided entering Turn 14 and Pérez retired. The stewards found Pérez wholly to blame and converted the 10-second sanction into a five-place grid drop at his next race because he could not serve it. Offer a choice between the evidence required for entitlement and the evidence required for sanction delivery. Treat the conversion as this documented exercise of discretion. Do not offer a universal time-to-grid conversion table. Later cases can introduce how penalty guidelines inform decisions or how review and appeal differ, provided the original and subsequent decisions and their dates are available for the selected incident.
