# Curriculum options 1–3 · Chassis, aero and suspension

Concept briefs for evaluating the proposed 14-module curriculum. Regulatory references pin the editions available on 23 November 2026. In a lesson, keep four labels distinct where applicable: FIA requirement, FIA design aim, teaching model, and Apex game output. Give each graph a unit and a text reading, each drag or scrub an equivalent keyboard control, and each animated comparison a paused or stepped view.

## 1. Active aero and the slipstream paradox

**Aha.** At the same gap, an Apex car can benefit from the tow on a straight and still carry a wake-related grip penalty into the corner. The real 2026 driver-adjustable wing configurations answer a different question about drag, downforce and operating zones. A regulation check and a wake experiment should be independently understandable.

**Sandbox.** Build on the existing Aero Lab. Freeze the gap and offset, predict what changes on corner entry, then toggle between straight and corner. Move the follower off-line or behind the model’s wake cutoff for a clean-air comparison. Show a separate corner/straight wing diagram with a marked FIA activation zone. In the rules exercise, the player can request Straight Mode only with the enabled flag and zone conditions satisfied. Section B distinguishes full activation from front-only partial activation in declared Low Grip Conditions and the corresponding Low Grip Activation Zones. Request outside the zone, or after the enabled signal clears, to show why a low-drag shape being mechanically possible does not make its use permissible. Overtake Mode is a separate electrical deployment concept.

**Readouts.** In the Apex panel, show gap in metres, lateral offset, normalized classic wake and usable tow, and relative grip with a 100% clean-air reference. At 10 m on the centreline and at the reference speed, the existing fixture is approximately 86% wake and 88% relative grip. The straight has approximately 86% usable tow, the corner 0%, with the same grip result under these held assumptions. Outside the 34 m centreline cutoff or at the 4 m lateral boundary, the classic estimate is zero wake and 100% grip. Display the wing state and legal eligibility in the rules panel. In the separate force experiment, 80% local airspeed gives 64% dynamic pressure at constant density. Force follows that ratio when area and the relevant force coefficient stay constant. The real wake can also change flow angle, separation and the effective coefficient. Keep the teaching experiment independent of the game's normalized wake percentage.

**Challenge.** At 10 m, predict which instrument changes first when the car reaches the corner, then recover clean-air grip with one control. In a second short card choose whether the car may request full, partial or no wing activation in an explicitly described zone and grip state. Score the reasoning on each card separately.

**Open decisions.** Start with a fictional zone strip or source a dated event-specific activation map and series instructions. Do not grade wing usage from a public speed trace alone. Skip real drag coefficients, lap-time gains and energy savings until a defensible source or clearly parameterized model is available. Validate that names, conditions and article references remain current before publishing. ## 2. Nimble car X-ray

**Aha.** A rule changing the package envelope gives designers room to trade packaging, rotating mass, floor behaviour and response. A smaller legal envelope is a measurement. Improved agility and raceability describe an aim and need their own evidence.

**Sandbox.** Use original schematic top and side silhouettes with the same scale and fixed axle-origin marker. Let the player overlay the preceding and 2026 envelopes, switch layers for wheels, floor and diffuser, and measure between the front and rear axle planes or from the centre plane to the body limit. The 2026 C2.3 checks use Legality Setup: a maximum 3,400 mm wheelbase and 950 mm either side for the applicable car envelope, with stated exclusions for tyres, wheel rims and defined attachments. Present 3,600 → 3,400 mm wheelbase and 2,000 → 1,900 mm width as the FIA’s published comparative envelope figures. Label retained 18-inch wheels and the FIA’s 25 mm front and 30 mm rear reductions in tyre tread width as comparative design figures. Avoid interpreting 1,900 mm as an absolute outside-tyre span. Use a cutaway to contrast the previous shaped tunnels with the FIA’s narrower floor and smaller diffuser concept. Avoid flattening the concept into a claim that the 2026 floor ceased generating ground effect.

**Readouts.** Give the learner a clearly labelled envelope ruler in millimetres. Attach issue and article to each legal dimension. For mass, show the Issue 21 C4.1 formula: 726 kg plus Nominal Tyre Mass in Sprint Qualifying and Qualifying, 724 kg plus Nominal Tyre Mass in other sessions. Show the declared Heat Hazard increment as a separate rule condition, and link to C4.7 for the tyre term. The 768 kg launch headline and projected aero reductions belong in a dated concept card. A qualitative overlay can show that FIA expected less dependence on the floor and reduced drag. A numeric force or agility trace would require team measurements or a labelled synthetic comparison that holds tyre grip, CG and aero constant. Wheelbase alone does not identify actual yaw inertia or corner speed.

**Challenge.** Give a player a 3,450 mm wheelbase drawing with bodywork reaching 945 mm from the centre plane and a tyre extending past it. Ask which C2.3 check fails and which needs the tyre exception. Then ask which evidence could support a claim that the car rotates faster. Accept an appropriately controlled measured response, with the legal check and performance question scored independently.

**Open decisions.** Begin with diagrams drawn from rule reference planes. Decide later whether specific team cars can be represented with verified dimensions and licensed visual references. Use exact 2025 article text if adding a prior-season scrutineering exercise instead of a concept-to-concept comparison. A real graph of downforce against ride height would need identified evidence and comparable test conditions. ## 3. Pushrod, pullrod and anti-dive

**Aha.** Pushrod and pullrod describe how an upright’s motion reaches the inboard rocker. Anti-dive describes how suspension geometry reacts to braking forces. A car can change the second without switching the first. Less nose dive also does not imply less total quasi-static front load transfer at fixed deceleration, mass, CG height and wheelbase.

**Sandbox.** Start with a front-view linkage and a wheel-travel slider. Trace the upright, rod, rocker, spring and damper. For the chosen bump convention, put the pushrod in compression and the pullrod in tension, with both operating the inboard rocker. Highlight how mounting and rocker placement change packaging. Then show a side-view braking experiment with fixed speed/deceleration inputs and selectable fixed anti-dive geometries. Draw different force paths through the arms and show different body-pitch responses for the same prescribed braking event. Preserve rod type in this second step. FIA C10.2 permits fixed kinematic geometries that alter contact-patch force reactions, such as anti-dive, and restricts active or feedback-based ride-height control. This does not grant arbitrary pickup placements or team-specific adjustments.

**Readouts.** Label rod force direction, approximate rocker motion, illustrative spring travel and body pitch. Show front axle load transfer m·|aₓ|·h/L in the simple flat, no-aero moment balance, with m, h, L and deceleration visible. With 800 kg, 1 g, 0.30 m CG height and 3.40 m wheelbase, the result is approximately 692 N shifted to the front in every anti-dive preset. Plot front load transfer next to independently modelled body pitch so the key distinction is visible. Prefer normalized pitch or two comparative curves unless a lumped mass/spring/damper model with specified assumptions is validated. Alpine-style or current team geometry, exact anti-dive percentages and tyre-lock probabilities should not be inferred from photographs.

**Challenge.** Present setup A and setup B under the same braking pulse. B dives less. Ask whether A and B have different quasi-static axle load transfer, then identify what geometry control could have changed the platform while retaining the same rod type. Transfer question: distinguish an unsprung bump disturbance from a sustained braking pulse using the labelled travel and pitch plots.

**Open decisions.** Choose the smallest credible side-view linkage for the force path and validate bump direction and pivot animation with a worked fixture. Decide whether to add transient pitch rates after the qualitative version. Where a named historical car is used, label contemporary technical interpretation as observation and avoid implying exact private pickup coordinates.
