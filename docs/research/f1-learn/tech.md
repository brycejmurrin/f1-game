# F1 technical regulations and lessons through 23 November 2026

## 2026 rules in force

- **Use the edition and the event.** The FIA's Formula 1 regulation index lists Section C Technical Issue 21, approved and published 19 October 2026, and Section B Sporting Issue 09, dated 1 October and posted 19 October. The separate Section C listing can surface Issue 20 as its latest entry. Section C specifies the car and hard constraints, while B7 and the FIA's event information govern how the systems operate at a particular circuit, session and grip level. The marked-up PDFs retain deleted text, so check visually when plain text appears to show two values. [FIA index](https://www.fia.com/regulation/category/2182) · [2026 Section C Issue 21](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [2026 Section B Issue 09](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf)
- **Chassis and tyres.** Maximum wheelbase is 3,400 mm, 200 mm shorter than the previous maximum. FIA design figures put width at 1,900 mm, down from 2,000 mm, and tyre tread widths down 25 mm at the front and 30 mm at the rear, retaining 18-inch wheels. The floor is narrower and flatter with a smaller diffuser, reducing dependence on peak ground-effect load. The FIA's projected reductions of up to 30% downforce and around 55% drag describe the concept. The operative minimum in C4.1 is 726 kg plus Nominal Tyre Mass for SQ/Q and 724 kg plus Nominal Tyre Mass in other sessions, subject to the declared Heat Hazard increase. Use C4.7 for how Nominal Tyre Mass is determined. The early 768 kg headline was a launch figure. The 2026 package also tightens crash requirements, including a two-stage front impact structure, revised side protection and stronger roll hoop. [FIA launch](https://www.fia.com/news/new-era-competition-fia-showcases-future-focused-formula-1-regulations-2026-and-beyond) · [FIA December 2025 explainer](https://www.fia.com/news/f1s-new-era-everything-you-need-know-about-how-fia-making-formula-1-more-competitive-more) · [2026 Section C](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf)
- **Active aero.** Standard-ECU-controlled front profiles and rear flap have high-downforce Corner Mode and low-drag Straight Mode. All drivers can use full activation in designated dry zones when enabled. In Low Grip the permitted partial state has the front wing in Straight Mode and rear in Corner Mode, in designated Low Grip zones. This is an efficiency and balance system. DRS was a rear-wing passing aid and the new passing entitlement is electrical Overtake. [2026 Section C, C3.10.10 and C3.11.6](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [2026 Section B, B7.1](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf)
- **Power unit.** Retains a 1.6 L 90-degree turbo V6. Removes the MGU-H and uses the kinetic MGU-K to recover and deploy electrical energy, rated at an absolute 350 kW electrical DC versus the previous 120 kW. The commonly reported roughly half-electric split describes peak output and varies over a lap. The ICE fuel energy flow ceiling is 3,000 MJ/h, with additional per-cylinder, low-rpm and part-load restrictions. This is a rate of fuel energy input. Compliance uses metered mass flow and FIA-measured fuel properties. The advanced sustainable fuel rules specify eligible components and certification, with non-food biomass, waste and renewable non-biological routes among the feedstock pathways. The cars still combust fuel. [FIA PU adoption, 16 August 2022](https://www.fia.com/news/world-motor-sport-council-approves-f1-2026-power-unit-regulations-and-updated-2022-2023) · [2026 Section C, C5 and C16](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf)
- **Power and energy are different limits.** The energy store has a 4 MJ usable state-of-charge swing on track. Baseline recharge at the CU-K HV DC bus is up to 8.5 MJ per lap, with a 7 MJ event allowance where braking/part-load recovery warrants it and a further qualifying/SQ reduction to a minimum of 4 MJ where hitting the limit would demand excessive harvesting. A maximum of 12 events may use the latter provision and no more than four may go below 5 MJ. Up to 0.5 MJ additional recharge may be eligible under Overtake rules. A battery can cycle energy in and out repeatedly in a single lap, which explains how the recharge ceiling can exceed the state-of-charge swing. Charging and discharge limits, torque and electrical efficiency are separate constraints. [2026 Section C C5.2](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [2026 Section B B7.2](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf)
- **Overtake, Boost and the deployment curve.** The normal statutory curve, combined with the 350 kW absolute cap, allows that full power through 290 km/h, declines to 100 kW at 340 km/h and reaches zero at 345 km/h. Overtake allows full power through 337.5 km/h and reaches zero at 355 km/h. Selected Race/Sprint sectors can be subject to a 250 kW plateau during power-limited periods, with different Low Grip curves. During the Race/Sprint, eligibility is based on a gap at the Detection Line and takes effect at the Activation Line. The FIA explainer describes one second, while B7.2 makes the actual threshold an event parameter. Overtake can also be enabled throughout lap-time-classified sessions and is disabled in Low Grip and under the safety car. Boost is a driver torque/power-demand override governed separately, with Low Grip use only permitted to inhibit the reduction of driver maximum power demand. [2026 Section C C5.2.7–8 and C5.12](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) · [2026 Section B B7.2](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_b_sporting_-_iss_09_-_2026-10-01.pdf)
- **Five 2026 PU suppliers.** Mercedes, Ferrari, Honda, Audi and Red Bull Ford. The 2024 launch's provisional six included Alpine, which subsequently moved to Mercedes power and gearboxes. Customer and works status matters for packaging and integration. Cadillac entered on Ferrari power. The manufacturer index described under ADUO measures internal combustion engine performance and cannot be used as a complete ranking of PU or outright car performance. [Official F1 PU explainer](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/2026-regulations-explained-all-you-necessitate-to-know-about-f1s-new-power-units.14jfv7a36905uDJDdNyfQd) · [Official Alpine supplier announcement](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/alpine-to-use-mercedes-power-units-and-gearbox-from-2026.5jzzbQeHx7aPT3hoP5hRtc)

**A recent event configuration.** The FIA's 17 November Las Vegas PU bulletin specified 6.0 MJ maximum qualifying recharge, 8.5 MJ in practice, and 8.0/8.5 MJ in the race with Overtake inactive/active. It specified 50 kW/s maximum PU power reduction, a 1.0 s Overtake detection gap at 5,299 m, and activation at 5,400 m. The alternative Race/Sprint curve applied from 300–500 m and 1,520–3,150 m. The bulletin also specifies distinct high-speed threshold and reset sectors. These parameters can be taken from a circuit sheet each weekend. [FIA Las Vegas PU bulletin](https://www.fia.com/system/files/decision-document/2026_las_vegas_grand_prix_-_power_unit_information.pdf)

## How the limits evolved

| Date | Evidence and implications |
| --- | --- |
| August 2022 and June 2024 | WMSC adopted the 2026 PU architecture in 2022 and the FIA showcased the integrated chassis and aero concept in June 2024. The early headline values are useful for original design intent. [2022 adoption](https://www.fia.com/news/world-motor-sport-council-approves-f1-2026-power-unit-regulations-and-updated-2022-2023) · [2024 showcase](https://www.fia.com/news/new-era-competition-fia-showcases-future-focused-formula-1-regulations-2026-and-beyond) |
| 27 February and 1 June 2026 | WMSC unanimously approved how the 16.0 geometric compression limit is tested. Through May the test was at ambient temperature. From 1 June through 31 December, both ambient and 130°C apply and arrangements that increase operating compression above 16.0 are prohibited. The 2027 published rule moves to 130°C with a specific procedure. [FIA amendment statement](https://www.fia.com/news/fia-statement-amendments-2026-f1-regulations) · [2026 C5.4.3](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) |
| 20–28 April, implemented from Miami | Using data from the first three events and driver input, refinements targeted reduced full-throttle harvesting and closing speeds. Qualifying recharge was reduced from 8 to 7 MJ in the announced package with an estimated 2–4 seconds maximum superclip per lap. Peak full-throttle harvesting rose from 250 to 350 kW in qualifying and races, and the number of events with lower qualifying allowance expanded from 8 to 12. Race Boost increment capped at +150 kW, or the existing power level if already higher, with race deployment retained up to 350 kW in designated acceleration zones and limited to 250 kW elsewhere under prescribed conditions. Wet measures reduced ERS torque, raised intermediate blanket temperature and simplified warning lights. Formation-lap energy counter reset addressed an inconsistency, and the low-power-start detection/automatic MGU-K assist and warning lights were trialled at Miami. [20 April FIA release](https://www.fia.com/news/refinements-2026-fia-formula-1-regulations-agreed-all-stakeholders) · [Issue 17](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_17_-_2026-04-28.pdf) |
| 8 May | The FIA reported no material issues or safety concerns at Miami following the first adjustments, while still reviewing starts and wet operation. A preliminary 2027 +50 kW ICE/−50 kW ERS idea was agreed in principle and then evolved. [FIA post-Miami statement](https://www.fia.com/news/f1-proposals-agreed-principle-further-evolutionary-changes-2026-fia-formula-1-regulations) |
| 10 June, followed by published 2027/28 rulemaking | Stakeholders agreed a staged ICE/ERS rebalance for the next two years to make qualifying more consistently flat-out. The published October 2027 Technical Issue 04 gives 3,150 MJ/h fuel energy flow, an absolute electrical ERS-K cap of 375 kW, and tighter permitted propulsion in specified modes (350 kW Overtake race/sprint, 300 kW ordinary race/sprint or power-limited SQ/Q, and 250 kW in designated race/sprint power-limited sectors). The absolute limit counts both energy directions, while the propulsion limits govern deployment. [FIA June agreement](https://www.fia.com/news/agreement-reached-proposed-regulatory-changes-2027-and-2028) · [2027 Section C Issue 04, C5.2](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2027_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_04_-_2026-10-19.pdf) |
| 8 October | The FIA's Sepang investigation documented formation-lap power loss when low-rpm operation interacted with a 250 kW reduction in certain sectors. It was found despite simulation and Belgian/Dutch FP1 testing. The configuration was revised before the delayed start, with another wet software update announced for the 25 October US GP. [FIA investigation](https://www.fia.com/news/fia-concludes-investigation-bahrain-gp-malaysia-formation-lap-software-bug) |
| 19 October | Section C Issue 21 clarified and expanded the sub-50 km/h positive-torque exceptions at standing starts. The FIA standard ECU may request the minimum-acceleration intervention, and a stall-prevention trigger after the start signal can allow positive torque up to that needed to meet PU torque demand. B7 permits wet-specific sector curves and constrained modes. [WMSC decisions](https://www.fia.com/news/rally-roadmaps-and-grassroots-affordability-focus-fia-world-motor-sport-council) · [Section C Issue 21](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_c_technical_-_iss_21_-_2026-10-19.pdf) |
| 10 November | ADUO review 3 (races 12–18) added zero homologation grants, as did review 2. Review 1 gave Mercedes one additional homologation in each of 2026 and 2027 (2–4% behind best ICE), and Audi, Ferrari and Honda two each year (>4%), with cost cap and dyno allowances. Actual performance index values confidential. [FIA ADUO results](https://api.fia.com/news/fia-announces-aduo-results-following-third-review-periods-2026) |
| 20 November | FIA, FOM, entrants and PU manufacturers unanimously agreed a 2030 framework based on a 2.1 L turbo V8, limited hybridisation, and a lighter chassis direction. The definitive framework was to go to the December WMSC meeting in Shanghai and remained subject to approval at this cutoff. [FIA 2030 framework](https://api.fia.com/news/v8-future-unanimous-agreement-reached-fias-2030-formula-1-power-unit-framework) |

## Prior eras and design lessons

| Reset | Mechanism and evidence | Lesson for a model or design brief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Late 1970s to 1983 | Ground-effect wing cars exploited low pressure beneath the floor. Following serious accidents and concern over escalating speeds, 1983 required flat floors between the inside tyre tangent lines and removed skirts. [Formula 1 retrospective](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/re-writing-the-f1-rule-book-part-1-from-wing-cars-to-flat-bottoms.EJZ73Lnn0c6EgcMKMAWOU) | Treat ground effect as geometry-sensitive and ride-height-sensitive. |
| 2009 | Slick tyres returned, KERS arrived, and broader chassis and wing geometry changes sought to reduce aerodynamic dominance and improve following. The Brawn/Toyota/Williams double diffuser exposed the leverage of interpretation and Brawn won both championships. [Formula 1 rule history](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/re-writing-the-f1-rulebook-part-4-cleaner-cars-kers-and-the-return-of-slicks.1NUrjfG83a6aE0QSuAkGK8) · [Formula 1 regulations history](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/the-key-regulation-changes-in-f1-history-and-the-teams-that-nailed-them.2iq8c5E6S1HOffT5PBLl6i) | Treat regulation intent, permitted geometry and competitive outcome as separate observations. |
| 2014 | The 2.4 L naturally aspirated V8 made way for a 1.6 L turbo V6 with MGU-K, MGU-H, store and controls. MGU-H managed turbo speed and could recover exhaust energy. Mercedes won eight consecutive constructors' championships from 2014 through 2021. [Official 2014 technical explainer](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/a-racing-revolution-understanding-2014s-technical-regulations.1bTbN3OAEksY00PVE62KvC) · [Formula 1 regulations history](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/the-key-regulation-changes-in-f1-history-and-the-teams-that-nailed-them.2iq8c5E6S1HOffT5PBLl6i) | Model integration, packaging, reliability and power delivery as compounding advantages. |
| 2017 | Wider cars, wider tyres and revised wings put emphasis on grip and faster lap times. The FIA retrospectively identified the 2014 and 2017 generations as having significant wake effects. [Official 2017 preview](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/a-new-dawn-rises-the-2017-f1-season-preview.42A2oCRJ20mOakcKcEEMIW) · [FIA 2026 explainer](https://www.fia.com/news/f1s-new-era-everything-you-need-know-about-how-fia-making-formula-1-more-competitive-more) | Peak lap performance and close-following quality are different objectives. |
| 2022–25 | Venturi-floor ground effect returned to improve wake, with vertical oscillation addressed by an FIA porpoising directive and subsequent 2023 floor changes. FIA's December 2025 retrospective estimated retained downforce at about 20 m behind improved from ~50% in the predecessor to ~80–85% at the start of 2022 and later fell to ~70%. It named front-wing endplates, inside front brake drums and the side of the floor as avenues by which outwash returned. Its ~90% for 2026 was an expectation. [FIA porpoising measures](https://api.fia.com/news/fia-takes-steps-reduce-porpoising-interests-safety) · [FIA 2026 wake retrospective](https://www.fia.com/news/f1s-new-era-everything-you-need-know-about-how-fia-making-formula-1-more-competitive-more) | Preserve provenance for estimated wake data. Test whether development progressively erodes following even when initial cars meet the design goal. |
| 2026 | Low-drag mode changes the energy needed along a straight. With a much bigger kinetic system and the MGU-H removed, braking distribution, state of charge, sector permissions, full-throttle harvesting and imposed power taper can all change how a battle unfolds. April's controls and the October Sepang investigation illustrate how software dictates on-track behaviour. [FIA Miami package](https://www.fia.com/news/refinements-2026-fia-formula-1-regulations-agreed-all-stakeholders) · [FIA Sepang report](https://www.fia.com/news/fia-concludes-investigation-bahrain-gp-malaysia-formation-lap-software-bug) | Model the car state (speed, charge, ICE demand), driver request, event-specific sector/zone, session, grip and race control as separate inputs. This permits authentic interactions, e.g. harvesting recovery followed by an electrical pass, and prevents all modes being represented as a single speed multiplier. |

For an implementation, retain dated configuration identifiers for replay and comparison. Useful boundary tests include 290, 337.5, 340, 345 and 355 km/h under the applicable curves, crossing detection and activation lines, changes into Low Grip or safety-car operation, and crossing the timing line or pit entry when a recharge budget resets. The race-start edge case requires separate logic for the launch, the automatic minimum-acceleration/stall exception and wet formation-lap ECU settings. Treat 2027 and the proposed 2030 hardware as independent future configurations.
